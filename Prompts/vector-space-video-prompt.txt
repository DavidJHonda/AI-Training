TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Teach the Markdown’s opening in order: each token starts with an embedding; layers change its numbers to reflect context; the updated numbers might not match any token’s starting numbers. Ask and answer how they can still represent meaning: an exact match isn’t necessary, because relationships between the numbers matter too. Introduce vector space as positions on a map where nearby positions can represent similar meanings. After the closest-city example, explicitly connect back: the new coordinates don’t match a city, but their position still tells us which city they’re near. Teach the map: latitude and longitude, the three cities, the two new positions, answered aloud: 38° north, 120° west is closest to Mountain View; 40° north, 76° west to New York City. Move to the drinks: seven dimensions in order, each row a vector; do not read the rows digit by digit; say Coke’s and Pepsi’s numbers are nearly the same and coffee’s differ.
