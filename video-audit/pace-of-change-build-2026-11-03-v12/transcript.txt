[0.00–4.32] The debate around artificial intelligence is getting louder by the day.
[4.92–5.64] The reason is simple.
[6.34–8.90] This technology is advancing at blazing speeds.
[9.72–10.98] Look at how it answers questions.
[11.72–16.60] In 2023, the model started typing the instant you hit enter, spitting out whatever came
[16.60–17.02] out first.
[17.78–23.54] By 2026, it can work on a hard problem quietly before it says a word, sometimes reasoning
[23.54–25.26] for minutes to find the correct answer.
[26.04–31.18] With images, asking for a picture of your dog back in 2023 might get you a result with a
[31.18–32.64] bonus leg and rabbit ears.
[33.26–38.92] The 2026 standard is photo-real images with perfectly readable signs and full video complete
[38.92–40.08] with sound and dialogue.
[40.94–43.66] Then there's the context window, its working memory.
[44.46–49.52] The free chatbots most people used in 2023 could only hold a few pages at once.
[49.94–53.52] If you pushed a long chat far enough, the beginning fell out of its head.
[54.20–60.48] Some models heading into 2026 can hold a million tokens, which means keeping a whole novel series
[60.48–62.68] in view at the exact same time.
[63.66–65.04] Finally, look at how it acts.
[65.86–69.20] Three years ago, it told you the steps to do a thing and wished you luck.
[69.80–72.12] By 2026, it does the thing.
[72.84–77.10] AI agents can actively book flights, build code, and fix errors while you watch.
[77.98–82.14] To understand why this is happening so quickly, look at the corporate landscape.
[82.84–87.12] The major AI companies are locked in an all-out race to dominate the industry.
[87.62–92.28] As a result of this intense competition, they release new models every couple of months.
[93.10–98.20] Each one of these releases is designed to push boundaries further, improving what the AI can
[98.20–102.48] actually do, how fast it operates, and how cost-effective it is to run.
[103.20–107.64] A limitation can disappear quickly, so today's no is not necessarily permanent.
[108.54–111.94] This graphic addresses the central question, why so fast?
[113.02–115.52] Three main concepts are driving this rapid acceleration.
[116.38–117.60] First is better training.
[118.42–123.68] AI learns from consuming data, and companies are feeding it more data of much higher quality.
[124.16–127.36] That directly translates into better, more accurate results.
[128.56–129.68] Second is more compute.
[130.48–135.98] Training these models requires massive physical infrastructure, specifically tens of thousands
[135.98–138.46] of specialized chips sitting in data centers.
[139.06–143.08] The AI companies are spending billions of dollars to expand this hardware footprint.
[144.66–146.98] Third, AI helps build AI.
[147.82–153.08] The most capable models available right now are actively being used by developers to help
[153.08–155.68] write the code for the next generation of models.
[156.64–158.72] Let's slow down and read that third concept again.
[160.18–163.12] AI is already helping people build better AI.
[164.10–167.78] Here are three distinct concepts the industry is actively striving toward.
[168.64–171.04] First is recursive self-improvement, or RSI.
[171.78–174.42] This happens when an AI builds a better version of itself.
[175.12–179.46] That new version then repeats the process, on its own, tightening the loop with little
[179.46–180.72] or no human direction.
[181.64–184.58] Next is artificial general intelligence, or AGI.
[185.06–189.82] This is the threshold where an AI matches human ability across many diverse kinds of work.
[190.68–195.56] Right now, there is no universally agreed upon definition or test to prove we have reached it.
[196.12–198.80] Finally, artificial super intelligence, or ASI.
[199.38–204.52] This is the stage where AI completely exceeds the best humans across nearly every cognitive field.
[205.32–207.80] It is important to note that these are goals in development.
[208.66–214.16] They represent theoretical targets the industry is aiming for, not guaranteed outcomes or current realities.
[214.92–217.92] AI keeps getting faster and more powerful.
[217.92–220.58] Nobody is sure where it stops.
