WHY THE OPENING WORKS
0:00–0:09.03: one split composition shows a hand assembling a complex geometric
tower while another hand repeatedly attempts to fit a square block into a round
hole. The positions/configurations change; this is action, not just a camera push.
The two sides visualize competence and elementary failure at the same time.
The comparison is understandable without reading a caption or a sample chat.
It supports the expert/six-year-old narration directly and has a comic edge
without depending on an actual child's appearance or a literal scientist.

0:09.03–0:12.07: the large question mark accompanies "How can the exact same tool
do both?" The picture changes when the narration turns from contrast to question.
The contrast establishes a problem before the next explanation begins.

0:28.83–0:52.40: the car analogy develops through road/steering, pedals, smoking
car, warning light, and looking under the hood. Each new shot corresponds to a
specific action, failure, or explanation in the narration. The sequence has more
visual development than holding one driving photo and one inspection photo.
Not every shot is literal model architecture: the stylized engine/network
illustration at 0:45–0:52 supports the car-to-AI analogy. Its subject alone is
not sufficient reason to reject it. Inspect its final use in context.

to the plain source prose, included the Be Smarter benefit in its intended strength,
preserved the goal sentence and all three exact questions, and delivered both
closing lines exactly. It needed two whole-sentence cuts and one accepted faithful
paraphrase. The selection therefore had a defensible narration rationale.

WHERE THE EARLIER DECISION WAS WEAK
1. The review declared itself transcript-and-still based and left motion/listening
   unperformed, yet the editing recommendation did not adequately account for
   the value of source animation. A four-second sample missed much of the
   opening's action. The best-of comparison concentrated on narration donors.
2. Selecting the narration base became selecting the default visual base. The
   strongest pictures across rolls were not systematically retained.
3. When the early edit's graphics disappointed, the recommendation moved toward
   replacing the whole first 55 seconds with new photographs, rather than first
   testing the first upload's useful opening and car scenes.
4. The project's photographic preference governs newly created supporting
   images. Edit Spec sections 8 and 8d separately say to preserve engaging,
   accurate source drawings/animations. That preference did not require
   converting effective source animation to photographs.
These are shortcomings in the editing judgment, not evidence that the opening
was unsuitable. The original review should have proposed this hybrid before
spending effort on a replacement visual system.
