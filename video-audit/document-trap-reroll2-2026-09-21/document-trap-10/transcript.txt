[0:55.90-0:57.90] This is the document trap.
[0:57.90-1:04.90] It happens when we assume that uploading a file means the AI has fully read every single page.
[1:04.90-1:12.90] When an AI processes a massive rulebook, it physically cannot fit that much text into its immediate processing area.
[1:12.90-1:18.90] Instead, it selects a few relevant pages and leaves the rest behind, including your tournament exceptions.
[1:18.90-1:23.90] AI models have a strictly limited working memory called a context window.
[1:23.90-1:29.90] The AI can only answer questions using the specific text that successfully reaches that window.
[1:29.90-1:34.90] If you upload a short one-page file, the whole text fits inside perfectly.
[1:34.90-1:40.90] But with a long document, the system has to actively search for the best fragments and filter the rest out.
[1:40.90-1:45.40] Anything failing to pass that initial search filter never reaches the AI.
[1:45.40-1:49.40] As far as the system is concerned, those missing pages do not exist.
[1:49.40-1:52.40] This chart shows how that filtering works.

[1:57.40-2:02.40] Next, search scans those pieces for keywords matching your question.
[2:02.40-2:07.40] Finally, load puts only the selected pieces into the context window.
[2:07.40-2:11.40] This three-step process explains the rulebook failure.
[2:11.40-2:19.40] The search successfully grabbed the chunks mentioning standard files, but the keyword match wasn't strong enough to flag the tournament exception.
