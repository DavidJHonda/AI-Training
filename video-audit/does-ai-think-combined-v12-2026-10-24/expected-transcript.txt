38.100–114.533 | roll 4 | base-chinese-and-analogy
There is a famous way to picture this problem. It is a thought experiment known as the Chinese Room. Imagine a person locked in a room who does not know a single word of Chinese. Slips of paper with Chinese characters are pushed under the door. The person inside cannot read the text, but they have a giant rule book that tells them exactly which symbols to send back. They match the symbols, write the corresponding characters, and slide perfect answers out under the door. This diagram shows exactly how the Chinese Room works step by step. In step one, people on the outside slide a note written in Chinese under the door. The text asks, how are you? In step two, the person inside looks at the paper. They can't read it, so they look up the entire phrase on a giant chart on the wall. In step three, they follow the rule book to find the required response. Write out the matching reply, meaning I'm fine, thank you, and slide it back to the people outside. To anyone outside, it looks like fluent Chinese conversation. A convincing answer doesn't prove understanding. The Chinese Room illustrates a distinction. Producing an answer and understanding it aren't necessarily the same thing. An LLM doesn't follow a literal rule book. It uses patterns learned during training to predict its response.
