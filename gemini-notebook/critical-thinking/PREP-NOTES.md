# Critical Thinking video preparation

Use the [lesson reading](../../lessons/critical-thinking.md) for narration and the [generation prompt](PROMPT.txt) for timing. Upload the five files in the [upload folder](upload/) and paste the prompt into video customization.

## Narration and edit cues

| Beat | Narration and picture |
| --- | --- |
| The headlines | Open with “Imagine seeing a headline that says you should eat your favorite food every day.” Read *Slim by Chocolate*, then *Has the world gone coco?*, then Shape magazine’s *Why You Must Eat Chocolate Daily*. Let students hear the shift from a study claiming faster weight loss to a magazine telling them what to eat. |
| The quiet hold | Once the narrator has attributed the weight-loss claim to the study, show “What’s your first reaction?” on its own and ask it once. Count five full seconds from the end of the question to the first student label. Keep that interval silent and keep both reactions off screen until it ends. |
| The two students | At the end of the hold, show the [Dallas Stars board](../../course-assets/critical-thinking/critical-thinking-two-reactions.jpg) in place of the text-only generation board. Read “Take it at face value” with the complete group-chat reaction, then “Question the claim” with the complete skeptical reaction. |
| Behind the Headlines | Introduce Bohannon and his objective. He invented the institute and built its website to make the study look credible. His team tracked 18 things in 15 people. Name weight, cholesterol, and sleep as examples of what they measured. Explain why the design gave him a chance to select a misleading result. |
| Critical Thinking Defined | Start with “Critical thinking means looking deeper for the truth.” Follow the TikTok and YouTube example through the questions about who made the claim and how they know. Give “I don’t know yet” time to land. |
| Knowledge Matters | Read the first-impression paragraph, including “So does what you want to believe.” Show the equation board. Pair Knowledge with spotting a missing detail, then Critical Thinking with examining assumptions, including your own, and weighing evidence. Read the result at the bottom. |
| What Critical Thinkers Ask | Use the habit-over-time transition. Read each heading with its prompt before moving on. **The Question** asks “What am I trying to understand or decide?” **The Evidence** asks “What am I basing this on? What am I assuming?” **The Alternatives** asks “What else could explain this? What else could I try?” |
| Close | Show the closing board. Say “Knowledge plus critical thinking.” Finish with “AI answers. You decide what to trust.” End the audio there. |

## Handoff and review

Save the raw generation as `Prompts/critical-thinking-reroll.mp4`, using the next unused reroll number if that name exists. During review, time the quiet hold from the audio, confirm the photo board appears with the student reactions, and listen through once with the picture hidden. Both reactions, the relationship on the equation board, all three card prompts, and the final decision should make sense through narration alone.
