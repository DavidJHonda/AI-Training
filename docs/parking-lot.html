    "Spot the Stronger Use of AI" TRY IT — Skills That Matter (2026-08-15)

    • Mechanic: mint InteractiveBox (variant: "try") holding an InnerCard; per scenario a UserBubble of the situation plus a QuizBlock with the statement "Which is the stronger use of AI?" and two options (one weak, one strong), each with feedback and the weak one with a hint. Title/lead verbatim: "Spot the Stronger Use of AI" / "Both approaches use AI. Only one applies the skills above. Pick the stronger one in each scenario." (the "skills above" phrasing is drawer language tied to the old SKILLS card grid and would need a rewrite wherever this lands).
    • Verbatim copy, scenario 1 (essay on a novel not fully read): situation "You have to write an essay about a novel you didn’t fully read." Weak: "Ask AI to write the essay. Submit it." hint "You’d hand in a finished essay, but the argument inside it wouldn’t be yours. What was the assignment actually for?" feedback "This skips the work of forming a view. The AI’s argument becomes yours by default." Strong: "Ask AI for a chapter map and likely themes, then read the key passages yourself, choose your argument, draft it, and use AI to critique the reasoning." feedback "This uses AI as a thinking partner. Direction, judgment, and ownership stay with you."
    • Verbatim copy, scenario 2 (gap year): situation "You’re trying to decide whether to take a gap year." Weak: "Ask AI ‘should I take a gap year?’ and go with whatever it suggests." hint "Whatever it answers, you’d just go along with it. Should a model you asked once make this call for you?" feedback "This outsources your decision." Strong: "Ask AI for the strongest case for and against, then ask it to find the weakest part of each argument and pressure-test both against your situation." feedback "This uses AI to widen and stress-test your thinking. The call still belongs to you, but it’s now a much better-informed one."
    • Verbatim copy, scenario 3 (code you don’t understand): situation "You’re using AI to fix code you don’t really understand." Weak: "Paste the error, paste in whatever AI gives you, run it. If it works, move on." hint "It might work this time. But you’d learn nothing, and you couldn’t tell whether the fix was actually right." feedback "This treats AI like a slot machine." Strong: "Paste the error, ask AI to explain why it’s happening, ask what the fix actually does, then test it and check that the explanation matches what you see." feedback "This builds the understanding you need to recognize the next problem yourself, and to catch a wrong answer when one shows up."
    • Verbatim copy, scenario 4 (club poster): situation "You need to design a poster for a club event." Weak: "Ask AI for ten poster ideas and pick the flashiest one." hint "Flashiest isn’t the same as right for your audience and your goal. Who decides what fits?" feedback "This trusts AI’s notion of ‘cool.’" Strong: "Ask AI for ten concepts, then choose based on your audience, the event goal, school rules, readability, and what’ll actually get students to show up." feedback "This uses AI to widen your options, then applies your taste, your knowledge of the audience, and the actual goal. Taste isn’t picking what looks coolest. It’s picking what fits the purpose."
