## Findings, in priority order

### 1. Two transition sentences promise too much

At approximately **2:41–2:49**, the narration says:

> Because sycophancy is a byproduct of how these models are trained, we have to change how we ask our questions to ensure we get honest answers.

At approximately **2:53–2:59**, it says:

> By giving the AI a clearer job and a standard to measure against, we prevent it from simply agreeing with us.

“Ensure” and “prevent” turn useful risk-reduction techniques into guarantees. That conflicts with the lesson's careful limits, including the video's own later statement that saved instructions are guidance rather than a permanent fix. This is a correction to the strategy's central promise, not a preference about tone.

**Recommended repair:** remove these two complete sentences at their surrounding silences. The remaining sequence is coherent: the problem has improved but remains → the table introduces five ways to fight it → the first move. The specific moves explain why they work, and the summary at 4:36 explains replacing validation with a concrete standard. No essential explanation needs a donor or a new generation.

The opening “built to be incredibly encouraging and polite” and the later “praise literally gets baked in” are broad, but the subsequent explanation explicitly says agreeable answers **can** feel better. I would not reroll the lesson over those phrases. A future narration revision could be more precise.

### 2. Gatsby comparison: improve readability, not speed

**0:23–1:22.90:** the portrait comparison board occupies approximately 674×667 pixels inside a 1280×720 frame. The two response columns have small body copy at full view. The full-resolution frame at 0:43 confirms this; contact-sheet thumbnails alone were not used to decide it.

The narration does valuable work throughout this minute: it reads the essay, contrasts the two replies, explains the absent thesis, and distinguishes positive feedback from empty approval. This is **not a minute of dead time** and should not be shortened merely for holding a board.

**Recommended repair:** retain the full-board opening and scenario, then zoom to the complete Flattery card and complete Useful Feedback card as each is discussed. Keep each photo, title, reply and result visible. Return to the full board for the takeaway. The tall cards permit a moderate useful zoom; verify the framing with a preview before committing to exact scale. Avoid cropping down to isolated text strips.

### 3. Opening diagram needs a small content correction

Around **0:15–0:23**, the supporting diagram labels the left side “RLHF Goal: Maximize User Satisfaction.” That presents one preference-related failure mode as the entire purpose of RLHF. A more accurate label is **“Training can reward agreeable answers.”** Preserve the comparison, arrow and reveal; this is a targeted label repair rather than a reason to replace the scene.

The “Agreement Score: 96%” is an illustrative device, not an attributed research statistic. I do not reject the scene merely because that number is unsourced. The issue is the categorical RLHF label. The right side's “Logical Stress Testing — Bypassed” is a visual metaphor for the missing evaluation, not evidence of an actual internal model diagnostic.

### 4. Keep the useful parts

- The complete Gatsby example gives students evidence to evaluate, rather than merely defining flattery.
- The positive-feedback qualification prevents the lesson from teaching that every compliment is suspicious.
- The three-stage training board connects human preferences to changed output patterns.
- The sycophancy example is brief; reading the entire displayed quotation would make it worse.
- Each of the five moves includes a weak prompt, a better prompt and a reason. The rubric, counterargument and saved-instruction limits are present.
- The Five Ways board already zooms to readable, complete active rows. Its supporting cutaways break up the explanation. There is no reason to replace these with a new set of still images solely for visual consistency.
- The correct closing lines appear on the canonical closing graphic; sampled frames show the push and settled hold.
