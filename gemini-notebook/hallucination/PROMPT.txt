“Second, find the source.”
“Third, check the match.”
“Hallucinations sound like every other AI answer.”
“When something doesn’t add up, trace the claim to its source.”

TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Read the playlist question and the full AI reply from the two cards. Give the reveal after the reply. Explain why Stanford, the sample size, the percentage and the volume recommendation make the answer believable. Define hallucination and explain how one invented detail can appear among real facts.

Teach the four reasons on Why Hallucinations Happen. AI learns from training text, predicts one token at a time and keeps trying to answer even when unsure. A probable answer can be false. Present these as reasons. Then speak the study comparison exactly as written. Go straight into the noticing paragraph, ending on the required sentence, and introduce Suspect a Hallucination? Leave out the deleted fabrication recap and the aside about misreading a real source.

Explain the three checking steps and apply each to the Stanford claim. Show how a student would look for the original paper and check whether it supports the 18% figure. If a real-world search comes up empty, the claim is still unverified.
