Core lesson: creative thinking means finding multiple possible paths before deciding which is worth taking. It is not limited to artistic work or being unusual. A creative idea must fit the real problem.

Open with someone saying “Good idea.” The student probably saw something others missed, connected ideas differently, or found a better route. Define creative thinking as seeing a problem differently, questioning the obvious, and finding a missed angle. Briefly mention Steve Jobs imagining simpler, more personal computers, but do not depict him.

Show board 1, “Who Thinks Creatively?”, legible as a whole, then teach each complete card:

1. A lawyer can arrange the same facts into a strategy others missed.
2. An entrepreneur notices an accepted frustration and asks whether there is a better solution.
3. An engineer treats failure as information, changes something, and tests another route.
4. A doctor considers another explanation, then tests it against evidence.

Explain that creativity is a thinking habit, not a job title. Similar AI tools and instructions often yield similar starting ideas. AI widens possibilities, but the student defines the problem, notices constraints, and chooses the direction.

Introduce one example: students spend half of lunch waiting in line. Carry it through board 2, “Four Ways to Think Creatively.” Show the full board first, then teach each complete card:

1. Generate before judging: create several options—preordering, pickup stations, staggered times, or faster payment—before choosing.
2. Ask “What if?”: change an assumption. What if students order before entering or use separate entrances?
3. Connect unrelated things: adapt useful patterns from restaurant preorders, airport lines, or theme-park wait estimates.
4. Step away, then return: save the ideas, switch activities, and come back ready to capture new connections.

After expanding options, choose an idea that fits the people and limits and can be tested without creating a larger problem. Creative thinking generates better possibilities; it does not select the strangest idea.

End on board 3 and say exactly: “AI widens the options.” “You still pick the better angle.” Hold it through the final frame.
