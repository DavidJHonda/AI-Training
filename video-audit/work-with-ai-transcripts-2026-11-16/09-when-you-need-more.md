# 9. When You Need More

`course-assets/when-you-need-more/when-you-need-more.mp4` · 2:48 · 453 words

**[0:00]** For many tasks, you open your AI app, ask a question, and keep the conversation going. But some tasks ask more of AI. You might need help working through a complicated plan, weighing options, or investigating information across sources. Depending on the app and your subscription, you may have three other choices for these demanding tasks. The model, thinking, and research. You won't need to change them every time. But knowing what they do helps you get a significantly better answer. When you use an AI app, an LLM powers its answers. Think of it as the engine inside the app. Choosing a model means choosing your engine. Different models have different capabilities. You start with the default, but can upgrade to a more capable one when needed.

**[0:46]** On the which model graphic, the everyday model is for quick questions like suggest a funny movie to watch tonight. The more capable model handles demanding tasks. For example, help me plan a summer business, including costs, pricing, and how many customers all need. Choosing a model changes your LLM, but thinking is a different variable. It doesn't change the engine, it changes how much time the AI spends working through your task. AI isn't thinking the way you do, but it spends more time working through the task before giving you an answer. This next board asks how much thinking. With less thinking, AI spends less time working through your task. it is useful for less complicated and routine work. For example, ask, turn these notes from our group meeting into a clear checklist.

**[1:34]** With more thinking, AI spends more time working through steps, weighing options, and checking its answer. It is useful for tasks that require analysis, like coding, planning, and problems with steps. For example, ask, help us divide this project among four people. We have one week, different schedules, and only two people know how to edit video.

**[1:56]** There is one more option, research. This mode gathers information from different websites and brings it together so you can compare your options. Regular chat can look things up on the internet too, but research is designed for a fuller comprehensive investigation across several sources simultaneously.

**[2:14]** For a regular chat, I live in Dallas. How long would it take me to drive to Texas A &M in College Station versus UT Austin? Use deep research to get information from many websites, compare what they say, and build a report that shows sources. For example, ask, I want to study physics and am considering Texas A &M and UT Austin. Compare their physics programs, undergraduate research opportunities, admissions requirements, and costs. Show your sources.

**[2:41]** Model. Thinking. Research. Use them when the work demands more.
