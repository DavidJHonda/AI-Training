Create a video for high school students from trust-trap.md and its three boards. Use one narrator throughout.

REQUIRED VERBATIM AUDIO
“We know AI is amazing, fantastic, and (insert your own superlative here). But how much should you trust it when you’re the one making a high-stakes decision?”
“The chatbot knows what you typed. It’s never seen how you act when you’re stuck, and it has no stake in which school you choose.”
“Which answer would you trust?”
“Trust Trap is giving a human-sounding answer the trust you would give someone who knows you.”
“Sounding human doesn’t make AI human.”
“For important decisions, talk to people who know you.”

TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Open with the two-sentence opener above. Bring the same college question to the dinner table and the chat window. Read both answers and both “The Truth” captions in full, in the Markdown’s order. Mom recommends Indiana because she’s seen where you felt comfortable and what happens when you need help. The chatbot recommends Michigan. Keep each recommendation attached to its speaker. Immediately after the comparison ask “Which answer would you trust?” Continue straight into how the AI answer could fit almost anyone and the definition of Trust Trap.

Explain why the advice can still feel personal. When AI says “I think” and “I feel,” you hear a person. Those words are generated. When something responds, your brain looks for a person behind it. Then explain how AI can gather facts, lay out options, challenge your thinking, and use what you share. Someone who knows you has shared experience and lives with the outcome. Talk the decision through and make the call. Finish with the two closing lines in order.

VOICE
Speak directly to one student. Read the parenthetical as a playful aside. Read both college replies in your own voice. Keep the reflection and its explanation together. Skip ELIZA history, school rankings of your own, the on-page activity, and requests to pause.

BOARDS AND VISUALS
Open on the first spoken line. Show Same Question. Different Answers. during the comparison, Why AI Feels Like Somebody during the explanation, and the closing board during the final two lines. Show each complete and uncropped. Use simple drawings between boards. No photos or photorealistic imagery. Illustrated, cartoon, and stylized people are allowed. Keep filenames, production labels, credits, and URLs out of the audio. End on “you” with nothing afterward.
