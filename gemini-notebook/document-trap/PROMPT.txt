Create a video for high school students from document-trap.md and the three attached boards. Use one narrator for the entire lesson.

REQUIRED VERBATIM AUDIO
“The answer wasn’t made up. It was incomplete.”
“In tournament games, how many fouls are allowed? Quote the rule and any exceptions.”
“Uploaded doesn’t mean fully read.”
“Ask for the rule and any exceptions.”

TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Start with the upcoming basketball tournament. The student uploads a 200-page league rulebook and asks how many fouls put them out of the game. Give the five-foul answer. Last year’s memory sends the student back to the book: five in this league’s regular season, six in its tournaments. The general rule was real, but the answer missed the exception.

Explain why uploading the whole file can still produce a partial answer. A short file may fit in full. With a long one, the system may search and give AI only the passages that match the question. Preserve “may.” The student mentioned the tournament in their story but left it out of the first question. Speak the revised tournament prompt once, then give this league’s six-foul answer.

Teach Four Tips for Working with Long Documents in board order: name the section using its heading, ask one focused question, share the passage or relevant section, and ask for an exact quote to check against the original. These moves help the student find and verify the rule; they cannot guarantee a complete answer. Connect to exceptions in leases, contracts, insurance policies, and financial-aid letters. End with the exact two closing lines.

VOICE
Keep the same natural speaking voice, pace, and tone for the story, quoted AI answer, tips, and close. Read the quotes yourself. Speak directly to one student in the Markdown’s plain language. Leave out the on-page quiz and discussion. Skip the former Split, Search, Load and RAG explanations.

BOARDS AND VISUALS
Open on the rulebook story without a title card. Show An Incomplete Answer, Four Tips for Working with Long Documents, and the closing board as their content is spoken. Show each complete and uncropped. The first upload preserves the title and banner; explain the missing tournament passage in the narration. Use simple drawn scenes between boards. No photos or photorealistic imagery. Illustrated, cartoon, and stylized people are allowed. Keep filenames, credits, and production directions out of the narration. Finish on the closing board with nothing spoken afterward.
