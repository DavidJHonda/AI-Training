[0.00–4.56] When you type a message to an AI, you use words, and it responds with words.
[5.28–10.76] Underneath, however, the AI splits your text into small mathematical pieces called tokens.
[11.82–17.44] Each token carries an embedding, a specific row of shifting numbers that represents its starting meaning.
[18.52–22.12] The immediate challenge is that human language is highly ambiguous.
[22.74–26.66] We routinely use the exact same words to describe entirely different things,
[26.96–28.60] depending entirely on their surroundings.
[29.32–30.20] First hurdle.
[30.98–32.88] Please turn on the light means brightness.
[33.60–35.94] The empty suitcase felt light means not heavy.
[36.84–42.10] For pronouns, in the cat was thirsty, so it drank the milk, it is the cat.
[42.68–46.50] In the milk was fresh, so the cat drank it, it points to the milk.
[47.58–48.42] Context determines meaning.
[49.30–55.36] Taking the fluid, context-dependent nature of human language and mapping it onto static mathematical tokens
[55.36–58.44] is the core obstacle any AI architecture must overcome.
[59.52–61.66] Our brains identify the correct meaning instantly.
[62.78–67.16] Early AI systems struggled with this because they read text strictly from left to right,
[67.56–69.12] processing only one word at a time.
[69.92–72.58] This diagram traces that older sequential method.
[73.28–76.90] The AI reads, the tired cat sat on the mat during the May rainstorm.
[77.66–80.34] Eventually, it reaches the sentence, it soon fell asleep.
[81.14–83.06] By the time it processes the word it,
[83.50–87.64] the AI needs to recall that the pronoun refers to the cat mentioned much earlier.
[88.38–92.80] Because of the distance between the words, the system loses track of that critical connection.
[93.84–97.70] Earlier AI often struggled to keep that connection, especially in longer passages.
[98.78–102.36] Reading sequentially breaks down when the words needed to shape a token's meaning
[102.36–104.72] are separated by long distances in the text.
[105.44–110.26] In 2017, researchers at Google published a paper called Attention is All You Need.
[110.82–115.38] It introduced a new architecture called the transformer, which is the T in chat GPT.
[116.16–119.04] The transformer can process the tokens in your message in parallel.
[119.76–123.68] This layout illustrates how the complete message arrives all at once.
[124.38–127.74] Every word in the sequence is visible to the system at the exact same time.
[128.42–131.76] Now, the token it draws directly on the token cat.
[131.76–134.52] The intervening words no longer act as a barrier.
[135.38–140.82] By seeing the entire message simultaneously, the AI stops trying to memorize a sequence,
[141.26–144.46] and instead maps the holistic relationships between words.
[145.26–148.96] The transformer relies on two specific mechanisms to make this happen.
[149.78–152.38] First is attention, shown in the left panel.
[152.94–155.78] The AI weighs information from relevant surrounding words
[155.78–158.26] and blends it straight into the token's numbers.
[159.08–160.32] Next is transformation.
[160.96–165.22] The system takes those blended numbers and runs them through learned patterns to process them
[165.22–170.64] further, taking IT after attention and turning it into IT with context.
[171.66–174.76] Attention and transformation work together to build meaning from context.
[175.76–178.98] Notice that both of these steps update the specific row of numbers
[178.98–181.14] representing the tokens in your active message.
[181.70–183.62] The model's learned weights stay fixed.
[184.56–188.46] In this panel, you can see how those mechanisms resolve our initial problems.
[188.46–194.30] The verbs turn on blend directly into the numbers for light, defining it as brightness.
[195.04–199.36] The word empty shifts those exact same starting numbers to represent a lack of weight.
[200.08–204.74] For the pronouns, the context of a thirsty animal directs IT back to the cat.
[205.28–207.76] The context of freshness points it back to the milk.
[208.60–211.66] Because a word's final numbers are completely driven by its surroundings,
[212.12–216.66] a literal word placed in a contradictory sentence has its meaning mathematically inverted.
[217.38–222.06] This continuous blending of context is how the AI interprets complex human nuances
[222.06–223.94] like sarcasm and idioms.
[224.76–226.20] Static tokens become dynamic.
[226.74–229.42] They gather their meaning entirely from the company they keep.
[230.22–231.80] There is one final constraint to solve.
[232.46–235.02] Analyzing everything in parallel destroys word order.
[235.48–237.24] And in language, order is essential.
[238.20–245.04] Dog bites man and man bites dog use the exact same tokens to describe entirely different events.
[245.04–248.82] Without position information, the model possesses the vocabulary
[248.82–251.66] but has no idea which action came first.
[252.06–253.10] The meaning scatters.
[253.82–256.24] The solution on the right is positional encoding.
[256.90–262.02] The architecture assigns a numerical stamp, one, two, three, to preserve the sequence.
[262.78–266.16] Positional encoding tells the transformer where every token belongs.
[266.66–270.28] By giving the AI a complete chronological map of the sentence,
[270.28–275.14] the system retains precise order of events without sacrificing the speed
[275.14–276.80] of analyzing everything at once.
