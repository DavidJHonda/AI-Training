“Temperature adjusts those probabilities.”

“AI answers vary.”

TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Answer the two-chat opening with the overview: Model & Thinking; What AI Receives; AI’s Choices. Cover model and thinking, prompt and available information, and techniques that give different tokens a chance. Transition directly to sampling and temperature. Continue without restarting the example or repeating the token-by-token explanation. Keep sampling concise: unfinished “You could name him”; Spot leads at 22%; qualified 100-tries meaning; five separate picks at the same blank with unchanged odds: Max, Spot, Buddy, Rex, Max. Spot once; another set could differ. Ask “Why does AI work this way?” Explain that other likely tokens add variety and can make answers more creative; a different token shapes what comes next. Connect sampling to temperature with the approved bridge and app names. Compare dog-name probabilities. Starting odds come from training and context. Lower temperature concentrates probability on leading choices: Spot 22% to 36%. Higher temperature spreads probability more widely: Spot 22% to 16%. Both use the same baseline. Compare 22%, 36%, 16%; Spot remains the most likely individual token; temperature changes its probability. Numbers are illustrative; Other combines the remaining vocabulary. Do not say every less-likely name gains at high temperature or loses at low. No cell-by-cell recital, guaranteed selection, retraining, or activity narration. Do not claim the two opening chats necessarily have identical context.
