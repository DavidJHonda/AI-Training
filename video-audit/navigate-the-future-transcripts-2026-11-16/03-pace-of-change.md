# 3. Pace of Change

`course-assets/pace-of-change/pace-of-change.mp4` · 3:42 · 572 words

**[0:00]** The debate around artificial intelligence is getting louder by the day. The reason is simple. This technology is advancing at blazing speeds. Look at how it answers questions. In 2023, the model started typing the instant you hit enter, spitting out whatever came out first. By 2026, it can work on a hard problem quietly before it says a word, sometimes reasoning for minutes to find the correct answer. With images, asking for a picture of your dog back in 2023 might get you a result with a bonus leg and rabbit ears. The 2026 standard is photo-real images with perfectly readable signs and full video complete with sound and dialogue. Then there's the context window. It's working memory. The free chatbots most people used in 2023 could only hold a few pages at once.

**[0:49]** If you pushed a long chat far enough, the beginning fell out of its head. Some models heading into 2026 can hold a million tokens, which means keeping a whole novel series in view at the exact same time. Finally, look at how it acts. Three years ago, it told you the steps to do a thing and wished you luck. By 2026, it does the thing. AI agents can actively book flights, build code, and fix errors while you watch.

**[1:18]** To understand why this is happening so quickly, look at the corporate landscape. The major AI companies are locked in an all-out race to dominate the industry. As a result of this intense competition, they release new models every couple of months. Each one of these releases is designed to push boundaries further, improving what the AI can actually do, how fast it operates, and how cost-effective it is to run. A limitation can disappear quickly, so today's no is not necessarily permanent. This graphic addresses the central question, why so fast?

**[1:53]** Three main concepts are driving this rapid acceleration. First is better training. AI learns from consuming data and companies are feeding it more data of much higher quality. That directly translates into better, more accurate results. Second is more compute. Training these models requires massive physical infrastructure. specifically tens of thousands of specialized chips sitting in data centers. The AI companies are spending billions of dollars to expand this hardware footprint.

**[2:24]** Third, AI helps build AI. The most capable models available right now are actively being used by developers to help write the code for the next generation of models. Let's slow down and read that third concept again.

**[2:40]** AI is already helping people build better AI. Here are three distinct concepts the industry is actively striving toward. First is recursive self-improvement or RSI. This happens when an AI builds a better version of itself. That new version then repeats the process on its own tightening the loop with little or no human direction.

**[3:01]** Next is artificial general intelligence or AGI. This is the threshold where an AI matches human ability across many diverse kinds of work. Right now, there is no universally agreed upon definition or test to prove we have reached it. Finally, artificial superintelligence, or ASI. This is the stage where AI completely exceeds the best humans across nearly every cognitive field. It is important to note that these are goals in development. They represent theoretical targets the industry is aiming for, not guaranteed outcomes or current realities. AI keeps getting faster and more powerful. Nobody is sure where it stops.
