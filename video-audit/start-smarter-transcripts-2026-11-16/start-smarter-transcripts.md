Second, it can work with unstructured data like everyday language, images, and messy handwriting with a lot more flexibility. Let's think about traditional software first. When you enter your password, the program checks the rule. If the password matches, the software follows the green path to a specific action. Then, open the app. But if it doesn't match, the system follows the red path. It automatically executes the else command, showing you a message that the password is wrong and asking you to try again. Written rules return the same result every time. AI learns patterns from examples during training and uses those patterns to answer you.

**[1:34]** People still write the code that trains and runs AI, but they don't write each answer.

Under the hood, doing the actual mathematical work is something called a large language model, or LLM. This graphic breaks down what that means. Large means the model was exposed to massive amounts of text and code. Language describes what it does. Reads, writes, summarizes, translates, and explains.

**[0:30]** A model predicts the likely output from learned patterns. ChatGPT is the app. The LLM is the engine. Before you ever type a prompt, the model goes through a phase called training. It digests enormous amounts of data to build a vast mental library of structural and cultural language patterns. Take a look at this board. It illustrates a pattern you picked up as a child. If I say peanut butter and, you instantly know the next word is jelly. You know it, and because of its training, the AI knows it too. These familiar sequences are everywhere. Twinkle, twinkle, little star. Once upon a time, you automatically fill in the blank. Better late than leads to never. The AI also learns complex structural patterns. By working through billions of examples, it maps how humans explain ideas, solve problems, write code, and make spelling errors.

**[1:23]** When you ask a question, the model uses those learned patterns and the words already there to work out what could come next. It chooses a likely next word, adds it, and repeats. The words already there change what might come next. Peanut butter and might lead to jelly. Peanut butter and banana might lead to sandwich. Your smartphone does something very similar when you write a text message. It suggests a word, you tap it, and then it calculates the next suggestion based on what you just added. This diagram shows how an LLM scales the process. It starts with, I want to buy peanut butter and, predicts jelly, adds it, then uses the new sentence to predict for, and finally lunch. It doesn't retrieve a pre-written file. It chooses a word, updates context, and predicts again.

**[2:10]** As the answer grows, every newly generated word gets pulled back into the engine. It actively becomes part of the mathematical context used to calculate the very next prediction. More than one word can fit, so the same starting text can lead to different answers. Training builds the patterns. The model uses those patterns to build your answer, one word at a time.
