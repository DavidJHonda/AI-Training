# 4. Where’s the Line?

`course-assets/wheres-the-line/wheres-the-line.mp4` · 3:21 · 544 words

**[0:00]** AI can do remarkable things, but as creators, we the people are responsible for how we use it. We exercise this by choosing which tasks to automate and identifying everyone affected by those choices. There's a word for this – ethics. It means thinking through what you should do and how your decisions affect other people. To see how this responsibility plays out in practice, we need to look at a real-world business model where these technical decisions carry serious consequences. DraftKings is an online platform where customers play casino games and make sports bets. The company earns money when customers lose. Traditional casinos operate the exact same way. However, some people struggle to control their gambling. The challenge is that gambling addiction is a recognized mental health condition, one that can seriously impact a person's finances, health, and relationships.

**[0:51]** When customers use DraftKings, they leave a digital record of their betting activity. AI can analyze that data to identify specific patterns in how often users play, the size of their bets, and how much money they lose. This opens up a thought experiment. What if AI could predict which users would gamble and lose more after receiving a special offer? The system could then trigger an email to those specific users saying, place a bet today and get bonus bets. Here is a different thought experiment. What if that same AI was used to identify users developing a gambling problem, allowing the company to offer help instead of promotions?

**[1:31]** When should DraftKings stop encouraging a customer to play if continued gambling could harm them? The New York Times investigated how the company navigated these exact choices, targeted promotions. The company developed AI to analyze user habits and permit which customers would lose more money after receiving free bets and bonuses. As for what they did with this system, DraftKings put this technology into active use and continued developing it. Customer protection. Employees developed a separate AI designed to analyze betting patterns and identify early signs of a gambling problem so the company could intervene. In this case, DraftKings chose not to put this predictive technology into use. DraftKings disputes the claim that they target users based on losses. The company states they already monitor risky behavior and determined there was insufficient evidence that the specific predictive system would be effective.

**[2:22]** AI will do exactly what it is asked to do, even if the end result is harming people. Making responsible choices requires four specific moves.

**[2:31]** The first move is to consider everyone affected. We have to look beyond the people who benefit from a system and identify who might be harmed, excluded, or pressured by it. The second move is to be clear with people. Explain exactly what the AI system does and how it affects user choices. The third move is to build in protection. Creators must test for harm, set rigid boundaries, and give users a way to challenge AI mistakes. Finally, own the outcome. Monitor what happens in the real world. If the consequences warrant it, you must be willing to change or completely stop the system. A responsible decision considers the people who live with it.

**[3:13]** We the people make the call. What should AI do? That's our responsibility.
