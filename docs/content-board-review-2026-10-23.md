# Course content board review

October 23, 2026

The strongest opportunity is to shorten boards that explain the same point several ways. The change to **What School Helps You Build** worked because Knowledge and Skills directly answer the lesson’s question. That same test can improve many other boards: what does this board need the student to understand or do?

Start with **Where AI Works Best, Evaluation Matters, Flattery Trap, and Make Your Move**. Keep the lesson structure and reuse current wording wherever possible. This is an editorial proposal; the course and its media have not been changed.

The review follows the current course navigation and implementation in [index.html](../index.html), supported by the lesson sources and board images. Guided illustration states are treated as steps in a demonstration, not as separate editorial boards. Openers, closing messages, quizzes, and TRY IT activities are outside the main cutting recommendations. Finish Smarter is primarily assessment and course utilities.

These are content reductions, not invitations to pack the same amount of text into fewer images. Deleted examples and duplicate explanations should usually disappear rather than move into surrounding prose.

## Where AI Works Best

The four strengths belong. They give students different ways to recognize a useful task for AI. The excess is inside each board.

Use **Editorial Explainer: Two-Card Full-Bleed**, with two strengths per board. Retaining the existing order avoids unnecessary restructuring:

1. Reshape Your Material / Explore Possibilities.
2. Find What Matters / Work Through Problems.

Below is a content sketch made from existing sentences and examples. It is not a finished board design.

| Strength | Retained explanation | One retained example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Reshape Your Material** | Give AI something you already have and ask for it in a different form. | Organize notes into a study guide. |
| **Explore Possibilities** | When you’re stuck or want more choices, ask AI for possibilities. You decide which ideas are worth taking further. | Brainstorm angles for an essay. |
| **Find What Matters** | Give AI a long document, a chapter, or several articles and tell it what you need to know. | Find what a scholarship application requires. |
| **Work Through Problems** | It can help break the problem into steps, compare possible approaches, and suggest what to try next. You decide what makes sense. | Figure out why your science experiment gave unexpected results. |

Keep **AI Helped Us Build This Course**. Its A+ / C- comparison makes task fit concrete. It serves a different purpose from the app-production example already removed from Your Home Base.

Explain the training connection once, using the existing paragraph after the strength boards. Keep its qualification: seeing many examples does not guarantee that the answer is right or fits the task. The lesson can then go from a concrete contrast, to four useful strengths, to the limits of those strengths without explaining training four extra times.

Source: [Where AI Works Best](../lessons/where-ai-works-best.md).

## How to make the other major cuts

**Evaluation Matters:** Keep “Is it right?” and “Is it good enough for what I need?” The overview must retain the Fix It loop back to checking. Keep Read / Understand / Validate. Reduce the five Dig Deeper tactics to three groups: check sources, including current information when needed; challenge the answer, including missing information; and check independently through calculation, testing, or a knowledgeable person. Keep task, stakes, and ability to judge as the criteria for deciding how much checking to do. These are essential distinctions even if they no longer occupy a separate board.

**Flattery Trap:** Preserve the weak/better prompt pairs. They show the student what to change. Cut the overlapping tactic, not the concrete examples that make the remaining tactics usable. The original comparison between praise and useful feedback should stay.

**Make Your Move:** Doctor, Electrician, and Designer would provide three contrasting settings. Give each one example of AI assistance and one example of human judgment or responsibility. Preserve the “may” in career forecasts. Keep Moves to Make as the destination; its actions matter more here than another explanation of why skills matter.

**Big Upside:** Retain AlphaFold and the Hassabis story. For the shorter breadth board, use Finding Cancer, Reading Aloud, and Flood Warnings from the existing material. Keep the limits attached to the health example and the role of human evaluation. The cut removes extra examples; it should not strengthen any scientific claim.

**Work Changes:** The assignment comparison repeats the same long task list in both columns. Show that list once, then make the contrast about who does the first pass and where the person spends their time. Keep Automate / Augment: it names a real distinction. A short reminder can reconnect the example to the four strengths without another recap board.

## Recommended order of work

Prototype **Where AI Works Best** first using the retained copy above. Then shorten **Evaluation Matters** and **Flattery Trap**, where the overlap is particularly clear. Follow with **Make Your Move**, **Big Upside**, and **Unexpected Results**, which offer straightforward example reductions.

Use existing headings, sentences, and examples before writing replacements. Where a board changes, revise only the transitions that depend on it. Preserve worked examples, important distinctions, and the student’s next action. Once a revised lesson is approved, align its narration and video boards so the shorter lesson does not retain the longer version in another format.
