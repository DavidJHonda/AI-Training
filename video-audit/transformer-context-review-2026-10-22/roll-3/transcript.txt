[0.00–5.32] When you type a message to an AI, like chat GPT, it doesn't process the sentence exactly
[5.32–6.50] the way humans do.
[7.18–12.14] Underneath the hood, the system splits your text into small, manageable pieces called
[12.14–12.50] tokens.
[13.32–15.42] Each of these tokens is assigned an embedding.
[16.10–20.26] You can think of an embedding as a specific row of numbers that represents the token's
[20.26–22.40] starting default meaning in isolation.
[23.30–25.64] But words rarely exist in isolation.
[26.32–31.46] The surrounding text, the context, shapes and determines what a specific word actually
[31.46–32.38] means in practice.
[33.60–36.60] This graphic displays two main problems context must solve.
[37.06–39.46] On the left, problem one, different meanings.
[40.24–42.76] Please turn on the light means brightness, but
[42.76–45.56] the empty suitcase felt light means not heavy.
[46.18–48.64] Moving to the right side, problem two, pronouns.
[49.54–54.06] In the cat was thirsty, so it drank the milk, it refers to the cat.
[54.62–59.00] In the milk was fresh, so the cat drank it, it points to the milk.
[59.62–63.92] In both cases, context ultimately determines which meaning the AI must select.
[64.56–67.88] As humans, our brains grasp the right meaning almost instantly,
[68.24–69.94] just by taking in the surrounding words.
[70.64–75.46] But for earlier AI systems, making these connections was a major mechanical hurdle.
[76.46–80.62] Earlier AI read text sequentially, moving left to right, one word at a time.
[81.36–84.14] Arriving at the pronoun it near the end of a passage,
[84.64–86.96] the system often lost track of earlier words.
[87.50–89.46] The connection back to cat was lost.
[90.32–91.54] Because of this linear approach,
[91.92–95.18] earlier models consistently failed to maintain conceptual connections
[95.18–97.26] in anything longer than a brief sentence.
[98.34–102.92] In 2017, researchers at Google published a paper called Attention is All You Need.
[103.44–106.48] It introduced a new architecture called the transformer.
[107.18–110.52] This structure became the foundation for large language models, and
[110.52–112.38] it is the T in chat GPT.
[113.74–115.80] Transformers read the same sentence differently.
[116.42–118.94] The complete message arrives and is processed in parallel.
[119.56–121.28] By seeing every word simultaneously,
[121.92–124.80] the AI draws information directly from earlier words.
[125.48–129.68] It instantly connects back to cat, skipping the words between them.
[130.36–133.80] Receiving the complete message at once eliminates the barrier of distance
[133.80–135.18] between connected concepts.
[136.06–139.12] But simply seeing all the words is only the first step.
[139.66–143.32] The AI must actively determine which surrounding words matter most,
[143.82–147.08] and then use that data to update its mathematical understanding.
[147.86–149.12] Two mechanisms do this work.
[150.04–152.72] First, attention weighs relevant surrounding words,
[153.10–156.38] drawing context from cat blending it into it.
[157.34–160.88] Then transformation applies learned patterns to refine those numbers.
[161.50–164.76] The data bars evolve into their final state with full context.
[165.86–168.16] Attention and transformation are paired systems.
[168.16–173.26] They work together continuously to synthesize the surrounding words into concrete meaning.
[174.16–177.54] It is helpful to clarify exactly what is changing during this process.
[178.28–181.62] The AI model's underlying learned weights are fixed and permanent.
[182.22–185.76] What actually changes and updates is the specific row of numbers
[185.76–188.10] representing the tokens in your current message.
[189.10–191.36] These updates resolve our original problems.
[191.98–195.52] On the left, turn on defines light as brightness,
[195.52–198.24] while empty defines it as not heavy.
[199.06–202.28] On the right, thirsty connects it back to the cat,
[202.78–204.80] while fresh maps it to the milk.
[205.60–208.36] Just as the word thirsty maps a pronoun to a cat,
[208.82–211.90] evaluating how every word interacts with the rest of the sentence
[211.90–214.68] allows the AI to map the complex relationships
[214.68–216.64] that form sarcasm and idioms.
[217.46–220.20] Ultimately, attention and transformation are the mechanisms
[220.20–222.98] that decipher which intended meaning fits your prompt.
[223.76–225.80] There is one final piece to this puzzle.
[226.62–229.20] Having the right words is useless without the correct order.
[229.76–232.12] The three tokens dog, bites, and man
[232.12–235.64] create vastly different meanings depending on how they are arranged.
[236.80–238.52] Transformers process everything in parallel,
[238.96–240.54] naturally lacking a sense of sequence.
[241.24–244.24] Without position information, words are just floating data.
[244.92–246.74] The solution is positional encoding.
[247.42–251.00] The system assigns a numerical position stamp to each token.
[251.62–253.56] Dog is locked to position one,
[254.20–256.56] bites to two, and man to three.
[257.52–260.16] Positional encoding tracks the entire sequence,
[260.46–263.72] telling the transformer exactly where every single token
[263.72–265.20] belongs in the broader sentence.
[266.06–268.14] This brings us back to the core mechanism
[268.14–270.50] driving modern AI text comprehension.
[271.12–274.44] As the researchers established, attention is all you need.
[275.12–277.56] By mathematically mapping the intricate relationships
[277.56–281.04] between words, AI successfully interprets your message.
