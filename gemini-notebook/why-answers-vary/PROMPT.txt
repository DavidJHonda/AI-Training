Create a video lesson from why-answers-vary.md, titled Why Answers Vary. Follow its order; consolidate repeated explanations.

REQUIRED VERBATIM AUDIO
Speak these lines exactly.
“You ask AI, “What should I name my new dog?” It suggests Spot. You start a new chat and ask the exact same question. This time, it suggests Max.”
“Why did the same question get a different answer?”
“Two techniques help explain AI’s choices: sampling and temperature.”
“A 22% probability means AI would pick Spot about 22 times out of 100 tries, on average, if the odds stay the same.”
“That’s called sampling.”
“In chat apps, it’s already set for you behind the scenes.”
“Spot remains the most likely individual token at all three settings. Temperature changes its probability.”
“AI answers vary.”
“Different inputs. Different odds. Different choices.”

TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Answer the two-chat opening with the overview: Model & Thinking; What AI Receives; AI’s Choices. Cover thinking as well as model selection, prompt and other information available to AI, and behind-the-scenes techniques that give different tokens a chance. Transition directly to sampling and temperature. Continue without restarting the example or repeating the token-by-token explanation. Keep sampling concise: unfinished “You could name him”; Spot leads at 22%; qualified 100-tries meaning; five separate picks at the same blank with unchanged odds: Max, Spot, Buddy, Rex, Max. Spot once; another set could differ. Ask “Why does AI work this way?” Explain that other likely tokens add variety and can make answers more creative; a different token shapes what comes next. Introduce temperature and its behind-the-scenes setting. Starting odds come from training and current context. Lower temperature concentrates odds: Spot 22% to 36%; less likely names lose probability. Higher temperature spreads the same starting odds: Spot 22% to 16%; less likely names gain probability. Compare 22%, 36%, 16%; Spot remains the most likely individual token; temperature changes its probability. Numbers are illustrative; Other combines the remaining vocabulary. No cell-by-cell recital, guaranteed selection, retraining, computation, or activity narration. Do not claim the two opening chats necessarily have identical context.
