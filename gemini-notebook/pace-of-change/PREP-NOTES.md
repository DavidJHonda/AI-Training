## Lesson progression

The lesson moves from concrete changes in AI capability, through what drives progress, to three future ideas and an uncertain endpoint.

1. Open with the pace-of-change hook, then compare ChatGPT in 2023 and 2026 across answering, images, context window, and doing. Cover both years for each row.
2. Explain the race between AI companies: frequent releases can improve capability, speed, or cost. Today's limitation need not be permanent.
3. Teach Why So Fast?: better training, more compute, and AI helping people write code for the next models. Omit the deleted claim that AI moves much faster than people on well-defined tasks.
4. Use “AI is already helping people build better AI” to connect today's development process to the future ideas.
5. Teach recursive self-improvement (RSI), artificial general intelligence (AGI), and artificial superintelligence (ASI) once each. Explain the repeating improvement loop, broad human-level ability and lack of an agreed definition or test, then ability beyond the best humans across nearly every cognitive field. These are three future ideas, not sequential stages or promises.
6. End with “AI keeps getting faster and more powerful.” and “Nobody is sure where it stops.” Nothing afterward.
