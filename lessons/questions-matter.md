## WORK WITH AI

# Questions Matter

AI can help with all kinds of work. But getting useful help starts with knowing what to ask.

For decades, technology has been making it easier and easier to get answers. The less time we spend chasing an answer, the more time we can spend asking the right question.

One example makes the whole idea click. Teachers have been handing out research assignments since the dawn of school. But look at how this changed between your grandparents, your parents, and you.

How answers got easier and faster

## Pre-Internet (Grandparents)

#### 📚 The library

1. Get to a library and search the card catalog

2. Track down the right books and periodicals on the shelves

3. Read until you find what you need, copying notes by hand

## Time to answer

Half a Saturday

## The Internet (Parents)

#### 🔍 Search

1. Run search after search, opening tab after tab

2. Judge which sites to trust and skim for the useful parts

3. Copy and paste the good pieces into a doc

## Time to answer

An hour or two

## Now (You)

#### 🤖 AI

1. Open your favorite AI app

2. Ask

3. The answer is on your screen

## Time to answer

Seconds

Half a day, then an hour, now seconds. AI makes answers cheap to get. That does not make humans less valuable. **It changes where value lives.**

## Pre-AI: Finding the Answer

Finding a good answer took work. You had to know where to look, what to trust, and what mattered.

## With AI: Asking the Right Question

AI can give you an answer in seconds. You still have to decide which questions to ask and whether the answer helps.

## What Makes a Good Question?

Asking good questions is one of humanity’s oldest skills. Socrates taught by doing almost nothing but asking. The scientific method doesn’t start with an answer; it starts with a question worth testing. Someone once said that with an hour to save the world, they’d spend the first 55 minutes finding the right question.

So what separates a good question from a weak one? Four qualities, and none of them mention AI.

1

⚖️

Open-Minded

You haven’t picked the answer in advance. A leading question isn’t research, it’s a request for backup.

## Weak

Homework doesn’t help students learn. Can you help me prove it?

## Better

What does the research actually say about homework and learning?

2

🔬

Specific

Give enough detail about your situation to get an answer that fits.

## Weak

How do I get better at sports?

## Better

I play basketball as a point guard. Sometimes I lose the ball against pressure. What drills fix that?

3

🧭

On Target

Ask about the problem you actually need to solve. The first question that comes to mind might miss it.

## Weak

What energy drinks work best for staying awake in class?

## Better

I keep falling asleep in first period, even when the class is interesting. What could be making me so tired, and what can I change?

4

💡

Open-Ended

Invite an explanation, not just a yes or no. Leave room for an answer you didn’t expect.

## Weak

Should I join the debate team?

## Better

What would joining debate add to my week, and what would I have to give up for it?

Answers got cheap. Questions didn’t.

Frame the problem. Ask the next better question.
