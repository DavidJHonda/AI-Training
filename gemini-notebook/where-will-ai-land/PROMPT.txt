TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Go straight from “What might we be missing?” to Hanoi in 1902. Tell the story in reveal order. There are rats in the new sewers. The city pays for tails. Thousands come in. Then people spot rats with no tails. Explain right away how clipping and releasing rats lets them survive and breed. Give the pays-once line time to land. Carry the story through rat farms, the end of the payments, and a mountain of tails with more rats than before. Explain why counting tails made the plan look successful.

Use the four-stories bridge. Give each expectation and outcome aloud, in board order. In 1995 Clifford Stoll doubted online shopping could compete with malls. Buying online became routine. In 1940 Henry Ford predicted a flying car. Everyday trips are still by road. SMS carried short messages over mobile networks. Its popularity surprised its creators, and young people helped make texting an everyday habit. Australia imported cane toads to eat sugarcane beetles. They barely controlled the pests, poisoned native animals, and spread. Skip extra SMS origin stories, GPS, highways, and the on-page activities.

Ask the AI question once. Answer immediately with the possibility that its biggest effect, good or bad, is still unimagined. Keep the practical advice about recognizing uncertainty, watching what people do, and building skills that last when forecasts change.
