Create a complete video lesson from where-will-ai-land.md. Hanoi comes first, then four unexpected results, then the AI comparison and curiosity close. The central idea is that actual results can differ sharply from what people expect.

TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Open directly with “In 1902, Hanoi had a problem.” Tell the sewer problem and bounty in reveal order. Explain the assumption: one tail per rat, so officials treated a tail as proof of a dead rat. Thousands of tails arrive; only then reveal living rats without tails. Explain clipping, release, continued breeding, and why a live rat can keep the supply going. Let the pays-once/pays-forever line land. Finish with rat farms, stopped payments, a mountain of tails, and more rats than before. Bridge directly to the four stories. For each, explain the original purpose, then the Unexpected Result: Text Messaging—early one-way network messages to subscribers became everyday communication, helped by young people; GPS—military positioning and navigation grew into rides, deliveries, and precise timing supporting phone networks, banks, and power grids; Cane Toads—imported to eat sugarcane beetles, they barely controlled pests, poisoned native animals, and spread across Australia; Wider Highways—Houston spent $2.8 billion widening the Katy Freeway to ease congestion, yet rush-hour trips grew 30% to 55% longer between 2011 and 2014, as more road space encouraged more driving. Those dates are both after widening; do not present the percentages as before-versus-after construction. Only after all four stories, connect unexpected results to AI: Optimists and Worriers could be wrong; nobody knows exactly where AI will land. Its biggest effect, good or bad, may still be unimagined. Deliver the uncertainty/pay-attention sentence, then the two-line close. Do not restore Online Shopping, Flying Cars, the deleted AI question or practical-advice paragraph. Skip quizzes and group activities.
