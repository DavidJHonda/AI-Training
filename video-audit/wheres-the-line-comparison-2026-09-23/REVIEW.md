### What the test showed

1. **The boards are doing narration work.** With the same prompt and the same required lines, the
   two rolls without images spoke one required line each; the two rolls with boards spoke seven
   and one. Both narration-only rolls dropped the email line and rewrote the closing line, and
   full-1 renamed the four moves. Without the board images anchoring the words, Notebook treats
   the Markdown as material to summarize and illustrate, then narrates its own illustrations
   ("This diagram shows…"). The board JPGs keep it reading the lesson's words.
2. **Long holds did not go away.** Notebook holds a picture for the length of a topic block
   whether or not a board is attached. Narration-only replaced our 38 s and 46 s board holds with
   its own 40 s and 59 s diagram holds, and its diagrams carry wrong names for the moves. The
   hold problem is an editing problem: break a board run with the roll's own drawn scenes from
   adjacent beats, or with donor drawings from other rolls of the same lesson (we now have four).
3. **Photographs came back.** Both narration-only rolls put a photograph or photoreal face on
   screen; the board-kit rolls did not.

### Decision proposed

Keep the board kit as the recipe. Retire the `wheres-the-line-narration` registry entry as a
recorded experiment (leave the files; mark the entry tested and rejected). Fix long holds in the
edit plan, not at upload: the pre-roll script's insertion marks now say when to cut *away* from a
board as well as when to bring it in.
