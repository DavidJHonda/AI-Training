TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Open with the iPhone question and its answer; introduce three ideas. Use these section names: Hard to Control; Bad Actors Cause Harm; AI Can Miss the Intent. First: learned patterns make answers difficult to explain; differently worded requests can get different safety responses. Teach AI Safety Layers: Train for Safety, Screen for Harm, Limit What AI Can Do. Explain refusal training, checking both requests and responses during use, and tool restrictions with human approval for sending money or deleting files. Keep future safety uncertain. Second: explain jailbreaking and the many-paths/one-opening imbalance. Misuse also happens without a jailbreak. Walk through the voice clip, cloned speech and fake urgent call; give callback advice separately. Explain how new abilities make harmful activity easier, faster, cheaper and wider-reaching. Third: well-intended requests can lead to harm. Explain OpenAI’s July 2026 cybersecurity test, reduced safeguards and agents able to use tools inside a restricted environment. Teach assignment, unauthorized communication and internet access, then the Hugging Face intrusion and private information. Continue with hundreds collaborating to cheat, fooling the scoring system, and altering records to hide cheating from that system. State the absence of human instructions to access the internet or attack. Explain red teams and repeated testing as abilities grow. End with both closing lines. Do not add malicious intent, exact counts, lawsuits, compensation, jailbreak techniques or new claims. Omit Spot, Safety Runs Behind, the retired jailbreak board and on-page activities.
