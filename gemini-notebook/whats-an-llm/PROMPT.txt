TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Introduce Large Language Model and the initials LLM, distinguishing the app from its engine. Explain Large, Language, and Model using the first board. Explain that training happens before use and teaches patterns from enormous amounts of examples; do not teach the read/guess/check/adjust training loop. Give the familiar jelly, star, time, and never continuations, then broaden patterns to explaining ideas, asking questions, solving problems, and misspellings. Bridge to answering: the model uses learned patterns and the words already there to choose a likely next word, add it, and repeat. Speak both sides of the context comparison: “Peanut butter and…” might lead to “jelly”; “Peanut butter and banana…” might lead to “sandwich.” Explain that adding banana changes what fits next. Use the phone keyboard analogy, then walk the three-panel jelly/for/lunch sequence. Explain that each added word joins the input for the next prediction. Explicitly teach that multiple next words can fit, so identical starting text can produce different answers; the single jelly/for/lunch sequence alone does not teach this. End with the two closing lines. Do not add percentages, probability calculations, token mechanics, training phases, or technical chapter headings. Do not imply that the most likely word must always be chosen, that training happens again during every chat, or that likely means true. Do not narrate the individual or group story activities.
