Global course evaluation: coverage record

October 9, 2026 · 58 included pages

Companion to the ranked evaluation. Videos and Group Exercises are excluded. Each row records the lesson’s job in the full sequence, individual practice, and editorial judgment. “Keep” means no major global-design change is recommended; it is not a claim that every factual statement, device layout, or external product workflow has been certified.

The current live navigation is authoritative. Embeddings was checked against the current eight-state drink demonstration after that component changed during the review. No activity durations below are measured student performance.

Start Smarter

Page Job and connection Individual practice or material Judgment
Welcome Establish tools, navigation, and the seven-section route. Makes expectations visible before the first lesson. Setup guidance rather than a learning test. The computer/free-account advice and school-account caveat are useful. Keep. No need to front-load more theory or another checklist.
Why Learn AI? Make AI literacy worth learning now. Introduces effort, skill, and agency, which the final skills section later develops. Multiply Your Effort is a motivational game. It explicitly says the multipliers are invented. Keep. Treat the game as an illustration, not evidence for a productivity claim.
What Is AI? Define AI through familiar uses and distinguish recommendation from generation. Establishes the kind of AI the course emphasizes. Set up a free ChatGPT account and try it. The instructions include parent permission and limits on personal details. Keep. The core is appropriately introductory. The “two kinds” close could say “two uses” to avoid sounding like an exhaustive taxonomy.
What’s an LLM? Give the first compact mental model: app versus engine, training, learned patterns, and the next-word loop. Build a Story makes continuation tangible. Comparing runs also introduces variation. Keep the recent reduction. The lab changes both human and AI contributions, so it illustrates an evolving conversation rather than isolating randomness experimentally.
Does AI Think? Separate human-sounding output from evidence of humanlike understanding. Prepares students for interpersonal trust traps. Twenty Questions shows useful behavior from clues. The debrief matters more than whether AI guesses correctly. Keep the nuanced close. Bring Mind Trap and the finish back into line with it, rather than strengthening this lesson’s claim.
In Your Hands Distinguish uncontrollable developments from useful personal action. Provides an emotional foundation for the later future section. Catch What’s In Your Hands repeats that distinction under time pressure. Keep. In a pilot, check whether speed helps engagement without becoming the thing slower readers are assessed on.
Beyond the New Average Explain why subject knowledge, skill, and school still matter when polished output becomes common. Spot the Mistake connects prior knowledge with checking answers. Browser inspection confirmed the incorrect $52 claim and correct $48 feedback. Keep. Its payoff is later visible in evaluation, Work Changes, and Career Explorer.
Learn with AI Turn early understanding into useful study behavior. Distinguishes working from supplied materials from open exploration. Build Your Course Notebook uses the seven-lesson packet, quizzes the learner, and asks them to open a citation. Then transfers to real class materials. Refine the transfer step with class-rule and privacy reminders. Preserve the early practical payoff and source check.

Work With AI

Page Job and connection Individual practice or material Judgment
Work With AI Opener Shift from knowing what AI is to using it deliberately. The map groups tool fit, better inputs, and checking outputs. Orientation only; no separate exercise needed. Keep. The next lesson should deliver the practical focus this opener promises.
AI is Different Explain the consequences of rules versus learned patterns. Builds on What’s an LLM? and leads to tool suitability. Make Sense of the Mess uses a supplied three-page notes PDF to demonstrate flexible interpretation. Change scope. Retain the contrast and lab; reduce repeated mechanics and the premature risk/guardrail tour. Main report recommendation 1.
Where AI Works Best Give four useful categories of AI work and boundaries around unsuitable tasks. Is AI a Good Fit? asks for task judgments. The four strengths return in Work Changes. Keep. Reusing the four categories later is useful application, not redundant teaching.
Your Home Base Encourage a home base and deliberate practice while distinguishing app ecosystems. Name That App tests product-name origins. Change practice. Ask the learner to justify a home-base choice and identify a task to practice. Preserve the transfer-across-apps message.
Questions Matter Teach students to choose a question that is open-minded, specific, on target, and open-ended. Ask a Better Question compares a leading question with a stronger one. Keep. It supplies the purpose before Prompting Matters supplies the brief. Include the learner’s own explanation of the difference if the lab is revised.
Prompting Matters Show how to supply situation, material, requested output, and manageable steps. Build a Rubric asks students to work with criteria and points, then evaluate a supplied essay. Keep. More demanding than the preceding question comparison, but it has a concrete product. Pilot the setup and scoring effort before cutting it.
Context Matters Explain the context window and the app features that can supply relevant information. Set Up Your First Project creates a bounded context used in a later coding lab. Keep. If the Dino lab moves, repair the forward reference. Preserve reminders to share only relevant information.
Evaluation Matters Provide an operational workflow: quick pass, decide whether deeper checking is needed, then use, fix, or walk away. Good to Go or Dig Deeper? exercises proportional scrutiny. Keep. Link the existing guide here and reuse it during later labs. It should serve as a tool, not another memorization requirement.
Critical Thinking Develop habits for evaluating evidence and considering other explanations beyond AI answers. Challenge the Claim includes correlation, survey wording, source quality, and insufficient evidence. Feedback often avoids both gullibility and blanket dismissal. Keep. This is the standard that future-prediction and energy examples should meet too.

Understand AI

Page Job and connection Individual practice or material Judgment
Understand AI Opener Explain why looking under the hood helps users. Previews a chain of related concepts. Orientation only. Keep. Avoid expanding its previews into another layer of definitions.
Training Distinguish pretraining, instruction tuning, and preference tuning through one recurring example. Train a Kitchen Helper connects chosen preferences to later replies and labels the simulation as pretend training. Keep. Dense relative to introductory lessons, but its ideas and activity share one job and have later payoff in bias and flattery.
AI is Math Explain probability and how a clue changes the possibilities. Sets up next-token distributions. Guess My Number makes conditional narrowing visible. Keep. Explicitly tie the lesson’s simple counting formula to equally likely outcomes if touching this copy; the coin example is the appropriate limited case.
Tokens Explain splitting text and token IDs before introducing embeddings. Guess the Token Count uses a stated tokenizer rather than pretending every model splits identically. Keep. Ten examples may be more than needed; pilot whether later items add a new case. Do not require memorizing exact splits.
Embeddings Distinguish an identifying number from a numerical profile that carries usable information. Adds dimensions only when needed. The current eight-state drink demo progressively reveals ratings; Build Your Own Vector asks learners to construct and recognize club profiles, including duplicate-profile feedback. Keep. Strong alignment between explanation and doing. The later vector-space lesson gives this work a second payoff.
Transformer Explain relationships among tokens, attention, transformation, and position information. Be the Transformer asks learners to use sentence clues to resolve meaning. Correct the processing model. Whole-sentence interpretation and causal token flow need to be distinguished. Keep the accessible examples and position contrast.
Layers Show repeated updates rather than one magical step. Extends attention using the same CAT/IT example. Written Brain Break claims and feedback were reviewed; the accompanying video was excluded. Correct the CAT/IT flow in concert with Transformer and Vector Space. Keep the deliberate light break. Fix “AI doesn’t hear anything” in the written feedback when editing it.
Vector Space Give geometric meaning to rows of numbers and relationships. Returns to drinks and the changing contextual representation. Guided maps carry the teaching. 2048 is explicitly a brain break and later becomes a build lab. Keep the lesson and break; correct the IT trajectory’s causal explanation. The familiar examples reduce unnecessary new material.
How AI Answers Assemble tokens, positions, embeddings, layers, the final representation, and the prediction loop. The dog-name guided sequence and Run the Prediction Loop directly exercise the mechanism. Keep. This is the technical sequence’s main payoff. Its preceding-token explanation should anchor the earlier diagrams.
One More Thing Explain why the highest probability is not a guarantee and how temperature affects choices. Then switches to computing scale. How Big Is 2 Quadrillion? practices the second topic through extreme comparisons. Change scope. Keep variation; consolidate computing scale in Data Centers, where it already appears. Main report recommendation 2.

Avoid Traps

Page Job and connection Individual practice or material Judgment
Avoid Traps Opener Organize failure modes into answers, user reactions, and incoming fakes. Orientation only; “read the water” is a useful organizing analogy. Keep. Do not turn this into a list students must memorize before seeing examples.
Hallucination Explain fabricated but fluent claims and the need to trace evidence. Build the Dino Game demonstrates useful code generation rather than claim checking. Change the practice slot. Keep the strong source-checking explanation and give students a source comparison to do. Preserve the game elsewhere.
Training Bias Explain skewed examples, defaults, blind spots, and shortcuts. Also introduces stale information and retrieval. Broaden the Picture compares entrepreneur lists and asks what is missing; it includes a branch if the first list is already varied. Keep the core and lab. Secondary trimming candidate: let Document Trap own the full RAG explanation and retain a short current-source bridge here.
Document Trap Explain why an uploaded document may yield an incomplete answer, building on context and retrieval. Help AI Find the Answer asks students to narrow requests, supply the passage, and ask for quotes and exceptions. Keep. A future refinement could give one actual excerpt to verify, replacing a prompt-choice item. The missing-exception skill deserves representation in the Final.
Mind Trap Separate fluent advice from the trust owed to people with knowledge of the student and shared stakes. Who Makes the Call? distinguishes movie choice, car purchase, and a personal school decision. Keep the calibrated scenarios. Revise the opening “doesn’t think” assertion to match Does AI Think?
Flattery Trap Explain why agreeable feedback can feel useful while failing to improve work. Builds on preference tuning. Make Feedback Useful changes an instruction and compares responses to the same prompt. Keep. Strong before/after application. Judge evidence and specificity, since an individual live model may already give good criticism.
Engagement Trap Teach a stopping point and distinguish useful conversation from continued use beyond the task. Where Does Your Time Go? makes personal time allocation concrete and labels assumptions around its projections. Keep. The personal exercise adds something different from the preceding traps. In a pilot, check whether money projections distract from the stopping-point habit.
Support Trap Distinguish helpful wording from support that changes the situation, with clear escalation when someone may be unsafe. Choose the Right Support uses increasing stakes and distinguishes ordinary venting, preparation, and real-world help. Keep. The distinctions and explanation of wrong choices are a strength. Include a transfer item in the Final without reproducing the distressing story.
Fake Trap Shift verification away from appearance toward source, context, and corroboration. Which Photo Is Real? demonstrates fallible visual judgment and explicitly warns that looking real is not proof. Refine the ending with a source-trail decision. Preserve the demonstration rather than pretending it already practices verification.

Embrace the Future

Page Job and connection Individual practice or material Judgment
Embrace the Future Opener Frame uncertainty, possible benefits and harms, and personal implications. Orientation only. Keep the balance. Its “nobody knows” language needs the following lessons to teach evaluation rather than suggest all forecasts deserve equal weight.
Loudest Voices Show credible disagreement and the limits of confident predictions. Did the Prediction Come True? has five “Wrong” answers, following more failed forecasts in the lesson. Change the evidence task. Judge assumptions and evidence or use mixed outcomes. Avoid spending more space on repetitive historical misses.
Pace of Change Show visible capability changes, drivers of progress, and distinctions among possible future developments. Build Your Own 2048 pays off the earlier game and demonstrates a new capability. Refine only if needed after higher-priority cuts. The future-concept cluster is the likely load pressure; the documented/hypothetical labels and build payoff are worth keeping.
Big Downside Explain why increasing capability can produce harmful outcomes despite safeguards. Spot the Downside applies six mechanisms to new situations. Change scope and organization. Three larger categories with fewer examples would reduce competing ideas. Define “agent” briefly before its major case study.
Big Upside Give concrete beneficial applications, centered on protein prediction, with additional examples in health and daily life. Explore a Protein Shape opens the public AlphaFold database and lets the student examine a prediction. Keep. The live lesson distinguishes predicted structures from experimentally determined ones. Keep that distinction in the lab’s “answers” language too.
Rise of Agents Distinguish answering from acting and explain a goal-plan-act-check loop with human responsibility. Be the Agent applies choices to a basketball highlight task. Keep. It has a clear job. Add a short earlier definition where Big Downside needs it; no major resequencing required.
Work Changes Apply AI’s four strengths to changed work and distinguish automation from augmentation. Returns to why knowledge matters. Turn Reviews into Recommendations asks for original-file checking, rare complaints, and a human choice among recommendations. Keep and strengthen as a practical course demonstration. Label the fictional dataset and add a brief student rationale, rather than another large capstone.
Data Centers Connect invisible computation with physical infrastructure, resources, and community effects. Compare the Energy Use gives scale comparisons, followed by a 40-quintillion celebration. Refine estimates and comparison boundaries. Remove or reframe the unsupported bonus number. Let this lesson own the computing-scale explanation duplicated in One More Thing.
Unexpected Results Show that technologies and incentives can produce unplanned results. Bridges uncertainty to curiosity. The Great Hanoi Rat Quiz mostly rewards story-detail recall. Change most questions to transfer of the incentive mechanism. Keep the memorable story and some humor.

Build Your Skills

Page Job and connection Individual practice or material Judgment
Build Your Skills Opener Return from uncertain social change to skills the student can build and retain. Orientation only. Keep. The career sequence that follows supplies meaningful action for this promise.
Next Level Moves Teach a continuing conversation: think, learn, find a start, and iterate. Extends the earlier prompting lessons. Build a Career Exploration Plan establishes a field and plan carried through later labs. Keep. The common project earns its length and gives the section continuity. Avoid replacing it with unrelated mini-exercises.
Know the App Distinguish model capability, thinking effort, and research depth without requiring paid access. Regular Chat or More Help? checks one binary choice; feedback explains the three different kinds of help. Refine a few questions to require the subtype decision. Keep “start with the default” and account-independent practice.
Honesty & Privacy Teach ownership of submitted work, school rules, and proportionate information sharing. Clean Up the Prompt practices catching private details. The two guides provide useful reference material. Keep. Add brief earlier reminders where students first transfer to real school materials; the full treatment can remain here.
Where’s the Line? Apply responsibility to who benefits, who may be harmed, transparency, safeguards, and outcomes. Does It Cross the Line? gives another perspective without grading contested choices as mechanically right or wrong. Keep. A short rationale could make thinking visible, but retain the open judgment. The Final should include one related scenario.
People Skills Explain listening, noticing, appreciation, and challenging ideas respectfully. Pays off the importance of relationships. Career roleplay gives feedback and repeats the hardest moment, with a connection to a real interaction. Keep. One of the better teaching-to-practice matches. Preserve the real-person step.
Creative Thinking Teach generation before judgment, new assumptions, connections, and returning after a break. Solve a Problem in Your Field asks the learner to originate ideas and improve a solution with AI. Keep. Learner ideas come first, which gives the section’s agency claim practical meaning.
Curious & Flexible Teach selective curiosity and testing whether a change actually improves familiar work. Prepare for Change in Your Field produces a source-aware forecast and watchlist. Refine the match. Keep the career continuity and uncertainty checks, but have students compare one actual alternative and decide whether to adopt it.
Make Your Move Turn prior skills and career exploration into a real next step while preserving flexibility. Make One Move creates a 30-day plan and begins action beyond the chat. Keep. It is an appropriate action-oriented destination. Do not bury it under a new lengthy final project.

Secondary items for the next editing pass

These do not justify new lessons or a larger course:

  • Training Bias / Document Trap: RAG is explained in both consecutive lessons. Keep a short lookup bridge in Training Bias and let Document Trap carry the mechanism. Stale information is already correctly labeled a separate limitation.
  • Tokens: retain the stated tokenizer context. Exact segmentation and IDs should remain illustrations, not general facts students must memorize.
  • Brain Break written feedback: “AI doesn’t hear anything. It reads the text you type” is too broad for a course that also discusses audio. Reject the joke without adding this inaccurate generalization. No video change was evaluated.
  • AI is Math: state the equal-likelihood assumption for the simple counting formula. That small qualification is enough for the fair-coin example.
  • Big Upside lab: keep “predicted structure” visible when describing the database result. The live teaching already makes this distinction well.
  • Final review links and tags: fx25 is the clearest wrong destination. Review the remaining links for the best explanatory destination, and synchronize old section labels with the current map. For example, the question about sampling variation could point more directly to One More Thing.
  • Lab time estimates: validate with students using the intended account types. Do not infer completion time from a step count or from how quickly an experienced adult can follow the instructions.

The next useful evidence is a student’s explanation and transfer attempt after a lesson. A longer editorial checklist would not replace that evidence.
