## WORK WITH AI

# Questions Matter

Every conversation with AI happens the same way: you ask a question, and AI gives an answer.

For decades, technology has been making it easier and easier to get answers. And the less time we spend chasing the answer, the more time we have for the half that’s still 100% ours: **asking the right question**.

One example makes the whole idea click. Teachers have been handing out research assignments since the dawn of school. But look at how this changed between your grandparents, your parents, and you.

How answers got cheap

## Pre-Internet (Grandparents)

#### 📚 The library

1. Get to a library and search the card catalog

2. Track down the right books and periodicals on the shelves

3. Read until you find what you need, copying notes by hand

## Time to answer

Half a Saturday

## The Internet (Parents)

#### 🔍 Search

1. Run search after search, opening tab after tab

2. Judge which sites to trust and skim for the useful parts

3. Copy and paste the good pieces into a doc

## Time to answer

An hour or two

## Now (You)

#### 🤖 AI

1. Open your favorite AI app

2. Ask

3. The answer is on your screen

## Time to answer

Seconds

Half a day, then an hour, now seconds. AI makes answers cheap to get. That does not make humans less valuable. **It changes where value lives.**

## Then: Finding the answer

Answers were scarce. The valuable skill was knowing where to look and how to uncover a reliable one.

## Now: Asking the right question

AI makes answers abundant. The valuable skill is deciding what to ask, providing the right context, and judging whether the answer helps.

**Answers became easier to get → Better questions became more valuable**

## What Makes a Good Question?

Asking good questions is one of humanity’s oldest skills. Socrates taught by doing almost nothing but asking. The scientific method doesn’t start with an answer; it starts with a question worth testing. Einstein supposedly said that with an hour to save the world, he’d spend the first 55 minutes finding the right question.

So what separates a good question from a weak one? Four qualities, and none of them mention AI.

1

⚖️

Open-Minded

You haven’t picked the answer in advance. A leading question isn’t research, it’s a request for backup.

## Bad

Homework doesn’t help students learn. Can you help me prove it?

## Better

What does the research actually say about homework and learning?

2

🔬

Specific

It says enough about your situation. A question without enough information returns an answer that helps with nothing.

## Bad

How do I get better at sports?

## Better

I play basketball as a point guard. Sometimes I lose the ball against pressure. What drills fix that?

3

🧭

On Target

Specific is about how much your question says. On target is about asking the right thing: the problem you’re actually trying to solve.

## Bad

What energy drinks work best for staying awake in class?

## Better

I keep falling asleep in first period no matter how interesting the class is. How do I fix my sleep schedule?

4

💡

Open-Ended

A yes-or-no question ends the conversation; an open-ended one starts it. Leave room for an answer you didn’t expect.

## Bad

Should I join the debate team?

## Better

What would joining debate add to my week, and what would I have to give up for it?

Answers got cheap. Questions didn’t.

Frame the problem. Ask the next better question.
