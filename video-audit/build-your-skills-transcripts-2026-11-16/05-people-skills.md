# 5. People Skills

`course-assets/people-skills/people-skills.mp4` · 2:21 · 365 words

**[0:00]** Your group just finished this semester's history project, but Maya notices a problem with one of the examples and starts suggesting a change. Before she can finish, someone interrupts. We are done. No more suggestions. Maya goes quiet, and the group moves on without addressing her point. You notice that Maya's expression and body language changed. She seems hurt. What would you say or do next? For your next move, say, Maya, we didn't hear the rest of your idea. Do you wanna finish?

**[0:29]** This simple question shows her input matters. Maya actually has a valid point. The third example they included doesn't match the assignment's instructions. By giving her the floor again, the group now possesses the exact information they need to improve their work. Her idea deserves to be heard, even if the group decides not to use it. What happened with Maya matters beyond this group project. People skills matter even more in the AI future. First, you'll stand out. As more people use AI, polished work becomes an expectation. How you work with people helps you stand out. Second, trust still matters. People choose teammates and leaders who listen, keep promises, and treat others well. Finally, connection matters. AI can help with the work. People still need to feel heard, understood, and valued.

**[1:18]** You can build these skills starting today. Let's walk through four ways to practice. Listen to understand. Do not plan your reply while the other person is talking. Ask one genuine follow-up question before offering your opinion.

**[1:32]** Notice what isn't being said. Pay attention to tone, hesitation, enthusiasm, and changes in behavior. If you're unsure what a change means, ask. Show people they matter. Remember what they tell you. Give specific appreciation and give people credit when an idea is theirs. Challenge ideas, not people. Address difficult things directly and calmly. Explain the concern and suggest a way forward. This example doesn't match the instructions. Could we try another? The group fixes the problem Maya noticed. What could you say to give her credit?

**[2:09]** AI can write it. It can't be the person in the room. Listen well, speak clearly, and be someone people want to work with.
