REQUIRED TEACHING SEQUENCE
Teach these points aloud, in this order. Showing a board does not replace explaining its content.

1. Begin with the Avengers exchange: you ask in words and receive words. How do those words become numbers AI can use? Briefly establish the exchange without reading the entire answer.
2. Explain why giving every whole word a unique number breaks down: people invent words, names, and slang, make typos, and use emojis and code. A fixed list of whole words cannot cover every input.
3. Introduce tokens as reusable pieces, which can be whole words or parts. Their collection is the vocabulary. Use un / belie / vable, then show how un can appear in other words.
4. Explain where the pieces come from BEFORE describing a chat: during setup, engineers choose how text will be split and the vocabulary size. A program analyzes text to build the vocabulary and assigns IDs. The same tokens and IDs are used during training and chatting. Include the source's vocabulary-size examples briefly.
5. Now explain what happens when you hit Send: type text, split it into tokens, look up their IDs. Use the cat example to establish that an ID identifies a token; it does not explain its meaning.
6. Walk through the verified examples without reading every ID. Explain that a token can include a leading space. In I ♥ AI, the chunks are I, space-plus-heart, and space-plus-AI: three tokens. SP is only the board's label for a space, not letters the tokenizer inserts. The displayed URL has eight tokens.
7. Complete the return journey: when AI replies, output token IDs are converted back into text pieces and joined into the answer you read. This explanation must be narrated.
8. End with these exact lines: “Words become numbers.” “That lets AI work with your language using math.” No narration afterward.
