# 6. Context Matters

`course-assets/context-matters/context-matters.mp4` · 3:51 · 649 words

**[0:00]** When you type 2 plus 2 on your phone's calculator, you always get 4. Same input, same answer. Every time. But artificial intelligence doesn't work that way. Consider this scenario. Two different people ask AI the exact same question. What car should I buy after I graduate from college? When Luke asks, ChatGPT responds, You haven't mentioned a strong vehicle preference, so I'd start with something versatile. A Jeep Cherokee could work well, but compare its price, reliability, insurance, and fuel costs before deciding. But when Nate types the exact same prompt into the same app, ChatGPT responds, Since you've told me you love pickup trucks, I'd consider a Ford Raptor. It matches your interests, but make sure its price, insurance, and fuel costs fit your post-college budget.

**[0:48]** Same question, different context, different answer. Different doesn't always mean wrong. With the right context, AI gives better answers. This happened because earlier in his chat, Nate said he loves pickup trucks. The AI recognized this past detail and used it to suggest the Ford Raptor. What do you call everything the model can see when answering your question? It's called the context window. Think of the context window as working memory. It has a strict capacity limit and it is not a physical storage space on a hard drive. This illustration shows the five specific sources that feed information into this working memory tray. Two of these come directly from your current active chat, the prompt you just typed, plus earlier messages and answers in that exact thread.

**[1:35]** The remaining three sources operate in the background, your personalization settings, saved memory, and specific projects. All these combined elements represent the totality of the information available for the AI to generate its current answer. Because we know exactly where the AI looks for information, you can intentionally load these areas to give the model a head start.

**[1:56]** The first tool is personalization. This allows you to tell the app what you're into and how you prefer your answers formatted. For example, you can give it the instruction, I'm learning to code. Explain things in plain language and explain new coding terms. Next is saved memory. The app keeps useful details from your previous conversations and brings those notes into the context window for future chats. You mentioned that you love pickup trucks. The app saves that detail and uses it when you ask about buying a vehicle. Finally, projects act as a dedicated space to bundle instructions, files, and ongoing chats together so you don't have to repeatedly explain your work. A summer job project holds your resume, the places you've applied, and instructions for helping you prepare for interviews.

**[2:42]** By setting these up, you don't have to start from scratch every time you ask a question. Now that we see exactly what feeds the memory, we also need to clearly outline what does not automatically get into the context window. Having your assignment open on your laptop doesn't mean AI can use it. Share the relevant material in the chat or use the app's tools to make it available. And don't assume AI has everything you said in another chat. Memory and projects can bring information forward, but you may still need to supply missing details. Before you ask, check. Does AI have the information it needs? Have you ever been deep in a long chat and the AI seems to completely forget something you told it earlier? Because the context window has a hard limit, older parts of the conversation can be shortened or left out entirely as the chat grows.

**[3:29]** If an important detail gets pushed out of the window, simply remind the AI to pull it back into the active conversation. When you are starting a different task, start a fresh chat. Ultimately, with the right context, AI gives better answers. Control the context. Control the quality.
