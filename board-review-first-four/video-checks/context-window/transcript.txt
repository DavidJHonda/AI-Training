[1:12.80-1:16.10] The glowing tray at the center is the context window.
[1:16.10-1:19.10] Think of this window as the model's working memory.
[1:19.10-1:25.10] These five pieces of information are everything the model can see right now when it formulates an answer.
[1:25.10-1:29.90] Two of these sources live and die entirely within a single active conversation.
[1:29.90-1:33.70] They include your current prompt, what you typed and attached this turn,
[1:33.70-1:38.10] and everything you and the AI said earlier in this specific chat thread.
[1:38.10-1:43.80] The next two sources follow you everywhere across the app, carrying over as you open new chats.
[1:43.80-1:51.00] Personalization is what you manually tell the app once, your name, your interests, and how you like your answers formatted.
[1:51.00-1:55.50] Saved memory is the app noticing details in your past chats that are worth keeping,
[1:55.50-1:57.80] building a record of them on its own.
[1:57.80-2:02.90] The underlying AI model isn't actually learning or permanently changing from what you type.
[2:02.90-2:08.50] It's the app itself keeping notes and placing them into the window where the model can see them.

[2:16.20-2:20.70] Instead of re-uploading your resume or re-explaining the work in every chat,
[2:20.70-2:26.60] a project holds those specific instructions and files so you can step right into that workflow.
[2:26.60-2:30.40] Everything else sits completely outside of the model's awareness.
[2:30.40-2:39.20] Older chats, unscent web pages, and local computer notes do not enter the window unless you explicitly bring them through one of those five doors.
[2:39.20-2:43.10] Understanding these five inputs puts you in charge of the system.
[2:43.10-2:48.00] You are the architect, deciding exactly what data the model is allowed to see.
[2:48.00-2:52.00] While the window feeds the model, it also has a strict physical limit.
[2:52.00-2:54.10] The working memory is not infinite.
[2:54.10-2:57.80] It can only hold a specific amount of information at one time.
[2:57.80-3:00.00] This creates the forgetting problem.
[3:00.00-3:06.40] When a conversation gets long enough, the oldest parts of the chat hit that capacity limit and fall out.
[3:06.40-3:13.20] To make room for the new prompts you type, apps must either summarize those oldest messages or drop them completely.
[3:13.20-3:21.20] This explains why an AI deep into a long chat might suddenly seem to forget a rule or a detail you established much earlier in the session.
[3:21.20-3:27.20] Because of this capacity limit, starting a completely fresh chat for a new task is often the smartest move.
[3:27.20-3:34.20] By managing what goes into the window and recognizing its limits, you take total control over the quality of the AI's answers.
