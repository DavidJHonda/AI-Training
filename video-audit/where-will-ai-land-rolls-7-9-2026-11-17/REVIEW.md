## Candidate notes

### -7

**Provisional verdict: REPAIR; recommended base.** No essential beat missing. The main required conceptual correction is “the actual path of reality will diverge entirely” at approximately 4:02–4:06. Uncertainty does not establish that every forecast will be wrong. The later claim about an unseen application/complication still communicates the requested good-or-bad surprise. The longer technical phrasing is an editorial weakness, not a missing teaching point.

Useful optional trims: 2:27–2:33, “The most valuable applications…” is an unsupported ranking; 3:23–3:33 adds “intricate networks” and “compounding reactions” without a concrete new example. Its introduction of camps as “utopian efficiency” and “economic collapse” at 3:54–4:01 is also a stronger caricature than the lesson needs. See the proposed combined cut below.

### -8

**Provisional verdict: REPAIR; second choice.** The central teaching and all four examples are present. Its exact closing words do not outweigh the added certainty and jargon. Remove or revise the blanket assertions at 1:17–1:30, 2:28–2:34, and the “almost certainly” AI forecast claim at 3:42–3:51. “Hijacking” at 1:50–2:01 goes beyond the approved SMS adoption story; the core teaching does not require that claim.

Visual correction independent of narration: at about 1:14–1:17 the generated graph rises from 100K to 650K rats. Those numbers are not in the lesson and are not spoken. Replace the graph, not the valid spoken statement that the rat problem grew. Photographic phone/toad/highway shots and chart/diagram styling are separate production repairs.

### -9

**Provisional verdict: REPAIR; third choice, most extensive cleanup.** No essential teaching missing; no failure for paraphrasing “A live rat pays forever” or the close. The problem is added categorical assertions at 1:31–1:42 (“almost certainly” creates problems), 3:37–3:47 (systems “consistently” defeat plans), 3:52–4:04 (“unbroken historical record”; forecasting “largely useless”), and 4:18–4:23 (future “will look vastly different”). Four selected examples do not establish those general rules. It also says “Because of that” twice in succession across the AI transition. “The same rule applies to biology” misleadingly follows a claim that end users decide how code is used.

The tailless reveal’s sampled frames at 0:40–0:48 show several rats with long tails, while the narration describes tailless rats. At 1:28–1:30, a mixed pile of whole rats and tails is weaker evidence for a pile of surrendered tails than -7’s desk. These are targeted visual replacements, not narration failures. Rebuild the cropped/highlighted board and the missing canonical close.
