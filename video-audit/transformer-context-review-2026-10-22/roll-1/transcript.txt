[0.00–4.04] When you type a prompt to an AI, it doesn't actually see words.
[4.90–9.44] Underneath the interface, it splits your text into small mathematical pieces called tokens.
[10.22–12.94] Each of these tokens is immediately assigned an embedding.
[13.28–17.50] This is a specific row of numbers that represents the token's starting base meaning.
[18.20–20.58] AI works strictly with these numeric tokens.
[21.16–23.98] But to make the exact mechanisms easier to follow today,
[24.40–27.32] we will use whole English words as stand-ins for those numbers.
[27.92–32.80] Because these starting numbers are static, a single token on its own is rigid.
[33.46–36.26] The AI must rely entirely on the surrounding context
[36.26–39.68] to shape that initial math into a final precise meaning.
[40.74–42.24] Context solves two major problems.
[42.96–43.90] First, different meanings.
[44.70–47.06] In please turn on the light, the word means brightness.
[47.70–51.28] Right below, in the empty suitcase felt light, it means not heavy.
[52.34–53.56] Second, ambiguous pronouns.
[54.28–57.10] On the right, it refers to the thirsty cat.
[57.70–59.72] Below, it points to the fresh milk.
[60.50–63.82] The surrounding words are the only mechanism that determines which meaning fits.
[64.70–67.58] Tracking those relationships is the central challenge for any language AI.
[68.28–71.34] Our human brains see the right meaning instantly from the surrounding text.
[72.02–75.72] Earlier AI models, however, had to read text strictly in order.
[76.46–78.92] This graphic illustrates how earlier AI read text.
[79.48–81.30] It followed a linear sequential path,
[81.30–84.80] moving rigidly from left to right, one single word at a time.
[85.48–88.14] By the time this sequential AI reached a pronoun like it,
[88.50–91.94] much later in a long passage, it often struggled to keep the connection
[91.94–94.00] back to the earlier reference word, cat.
[94.80–97.10] Reading linearly breaks down in complex passages,
[97.68–100.68] meaning relies on distant relationships across a sentence,
[101.00–102.98] not just the word that immediately precedes it.
[103.58–107.04] In 2017, eight researchers at Google published a paper called
[107.04–108.46] Attention is All You Need.
[108.90–110.84] This introduced the transformer architecture.
[110.84–115.40] The structure used in modern large language models and the T in chat GBT.
[115.92–118.10] Here, we see how a transformer reads a sentence.
[118.78–121.76] Instead of reading sequentially, the complete message arrives
[121.76–123.20] and is processed in parallel.
[123.86–127.08] Every single word is visible to the system at the exact same time.
[127.74–131.06] Because the whole message is present, a later word like it
[131.06–134.64] can draw information directly from an earlier word like cat,
[135.22–137.80] completely ignoring the several words sitting in between them.
[137.80–141.26] Processing in parallel removes the barrier of distance.
[141.84–145.52] It gives the AI immediate access to the true contextual map of the text.
[146.54–148.98] Taking in the word simultaneously is only the first step.
[149.48–152.96] The AI still has to actively calculate which words matter to one another
[152.96–154.64] to update their underlying numbers.
[155.42–157.52] This chart shows the first mechanism, attention.
[158.28–161.16] The model weighs information from relevant surrounding words
[161.16–164.14] and blends that data directly into the target token's numbers.
[165.10–165.96] Next is transformation.
[166.68–170.78] Here, the AI uses its learned patterns to further process those blended numbers,
[171.28–173.30] reshaping the mathematical identity of the token.
[174.00–176.60] It is important to note that the massive learned weights
[176.60–179.42] inside the AI model stay completely fixed during this step.
[179.90–182.32] What actually changes is the specific role of numbers
[182.32–184.58] representing each individual token in your message.
[185.54–188.38] Attention and transformation work together as an active engine,
[188.84–191.54] building specific meaning from raw context on the fly.
[192.20–193.98] We're turning to our chart on different meanings.
[193.98–195.74] We can see this engine at work.
[196.40–199.72] The surrounding words turn on, provide the exact attention data
[199.72–202.50] the model needs to update the target numbers toward brightness,
[202.90–205.74] while the word empty shifts those numbers toward not heavy.
[206.48–210.54] Looking at the pronouns panel, attention connects the vague word it backward.
[211.00–213.38] It pulls data from the thirsty cat in the first case
[213.38–216.70] and the milk in the second, locking in what the pronoun refers to.
[217.26–221.68] This mathematical synthesis of surrounding context is how the AI successfully locks
[221.68–224.28] in the correct, nuanced meaning of human phrasing.
[224.82–227.38] While attention resolves ambiguous words and pronouns,
[228.08–232.36] processing all this text in parallel creates a new, critical problem,
[233.02–234.30] losing track of word order.
[235.22–238.50] Take the phrases dog bites man and man bites dog.
[239.18–243.18] Without sequence data, these identical tokens just float in the model.
[243.84–246.92] The AI has the words, but cannot tell which came first.
[247.88–249.84] The solution is positional encoding.
[250.48–255.08] The AI stamps every token with a position number, preserving its sequential place.
[256.12–259.42] Positional encoding provides the final necessary piece of architecture.
[260.12–264.46] It tells the transformer exactly where every token belongs in the structure of the narrative.
[265.68–268.24] Modern AI does not read text the way humans do.
[268.66–272.26] It relies on complex mathematical relationships between tokens.
[272.94–275.58] By weighing and blending these numeric relationships,
[275.58–280.02] the AI turns a scattered pile of words into cohesive understanding.
[281.06–282.92] Truly, attention is all you need.
