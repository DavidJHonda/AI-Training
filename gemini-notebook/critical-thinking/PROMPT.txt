Create a single-narrator video for high-school students from critical-thinking.md. Follow the lesson in order and use its wording.

REQUIRED VERBATIM AUDIO
“What’s your first reaction?”
“Critical thinking means looking deeper for the truth.”
“What you know shapes your first reaction. So does what you want to believe.”
“You can decide what to believe and explain why.”
“Knowledge + Critical Thinking”
“AI answers. You decide what to trust.”

TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Open with the favorite-food hook. Read the 2015 headlines in order: Slim by Chocolate, Has the world gone coco?, then Shape magazine’s Why You Must Eat Chocolate Daily. Trace them to the one study and attribute the faster-weight-loss claim to it. Ask the first-reaction question once. Show only that question for five full seconds of silence after the narrator finishes. Then read “Take it at face value” and the first student’s complete reaction, followed by “Question the claim” and the second student’s complete reaction. Only now introduce journalist John Bohannon, his purpose, and the institute and website he invented. His team tracked 18 things in 15 people. Weight, cholesterol, and sleep were measurements. Explain why that design let him choose a headline-worthy result even if it arose by chance. Move from convincing TikTok and YouTube claims to asking who made a claim and how they know. Keep “I don’t know yet.” In Knowledge Matters, explain how knowledge and wanting something to be true can both shape a first impression. Speak both parts of the Knowledge + Critical Thinking board and their shared conclusion. Use the lesson’s habit-over-time transition. On What Critical Thinkers Ask, read each heading immediately followed by its full question text: The Question, The Evidence, The Alternatives. Finish with the two closing lines.

VOICE
Read conversationally. Give each student a distinct delivery and let the skeptical student sound genuinely torn. Say “plus” for the symbol. Avoid dietary advice, invented statistics, “framework,” and “leverage.”

BOARDS AND VISUALS
Open directly on the story. Use headline typography. During the quiet hold, keep both reactions hidden. Then show the complete text-only reaction board for the editor’s Dallas Stars board replacement. Show the equation, three-card board, and close whole and uncropped, timed to their narration. Use drawn scenes between boards. No photos or photorealistic imagery. Illustrated, cartoon, and stylized people are allowed. Keep production labels and URLs unspoken. End on “AI answers. You decide what to trust.” with nothing after it.
