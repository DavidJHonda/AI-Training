## Lesson progression

An answer can be wrong or simply unsuitable. Review it first, check more carefully when the task or stakes warrant it, then decide whether to use, fix, or leave it.

| Beat | Required teaching / visual |
| --- | --- |
| Opening | Confident errors; joke, explanation and plan examples; why evaluation matters; both rightness and usefulness. |
| Overview | Do a Quick Review → Take a Closer Look → Make Your Move. Middle step is conditional. Use the three-step overview, not the retired branching flowchart. |
| Step 1 | Purple Do a Quick Review: Read, Understand, Validate. Keep all three explanations and the simpler-paragraph example. |
| Bridge | “A quick review is often enough.” Then “When more is riding on the answer, or you’re not sure it’s right, take a closer look.” |
| Step 2: when | Blue Take a Closer Look: task and stakes in separate white cards. Factual answer / draft / plan, then movie / scholarship / injury. |
| Bridge within board | “The more that depends on the answer, the more carefully you should check it.” |
| Step 2: how | Check the Sources (including current information); Challenge the Answer (opposing argument and omissions); Check It Yourself. These are choices of checks, not three mandatory sequential steps. |
| Step 3 | Teal Make Your Move: Use It, Fix It, Walk Away. Keep checking the revised answer before using it. |
| Close | “The tool answers. You evaluate.” Then “Review the answer. Take a closer look when needed. Decide what to do next.” |
