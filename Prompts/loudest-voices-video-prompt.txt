Focus: the people who know AI best don't know where it's going — where AI will be in ten years is a bet, and you decide which voices to listen to.

Open on the noise: opinions about AI are everywhere — YouTube, TikTok, the news, your family, your friends — and they all contradict each other. Who do you believe? Draw the noise as a teen surrounded by speech bubbles; write no text inside them.

Then the move: let's ask the people who make AI for a living. Same field. Same evidence. Opposite bets. Board 1, held long — walk all three columns in order. The Optimist, Dario Amodei, who built GPT-2 and GPT-3 and founded Anthropic: AI-enabled biology could compress fifty to a hundred years of progress into five to ten. But he admits humanity is about to be handed almost unimaginable power, and it's unclear we're mature enough to wield it. The Worrier, Geoffrey Hinton, Nobel Prize winner who left Google to warn people: we're making new kinds of beings, we give them goals, and we don't know what other goals they'll derive. But he admits AI could catch cancer earlier, which could mean fewer people die. Do not add a numerical estimate of lives saved. The Skeptic, Yann LeCun, Turing Award winner: LLMs are a dead end for superintelligence, with a more superficial understanding of the world than a house cat. But he admits there are risks — we build it, so we control it.

Then the tell, its own beat: all three admit the other side might be right. The people who know AI best don't know where it's going. Required narration, exactly: "Where AI will be in ten years is a bet."

Then history: this has happened before. Every big technology arrives with confident predictions from the smartest people around, and those predictions miss in both directions. Board 2, walk all four cards: the astronomer who said nobody would shop online — anyone heard of Amazon? Ballmer giving the iPhone no chance. Then why they were wrong, short and clean: the future is hard to predict. A technology becomes the future only when people change their habits around it. Machines improve fast. Habits change at human speed.
