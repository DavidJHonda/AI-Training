Focus: you hear about AI constantly, so what is it, really? The useful starting point: AI is software built to do things that used to take a human brain — and it comes in two kinds. Then image 1, the two types — walk both columns: Recommendation AI sorts and recommends, choosing from what already exists — the next show on Netflix, the next song on Spotify, the fastest route on Maps — and it never makes anything new. Generative AI makes something that didn't exist: it writes the email, drafts the essay, makes the image, codes the website. When people picture AI they usually mean this, and it's what this course is about. Then image 2, the same task handled by each — animate this as a two-beat story: ask "what movie should I watch tonight?" and recommendation AI just moves to the next title on its list, with no idea why you skipped the first; generative AI composes a reply, and when you say you've already seen it at a friend's, it takes your situation and composes something new — and it's still in the conversation. Then the name game: you'll hear a pile of names — AI, generative AI, ChatGPT, LLM — but don't let the terminology trip you up. For the rest of this course, AI means the generative kind you experience with ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini — speak all three names. These tools are LLMs, short for Large Language Model, and the narrator must say the analogy aloud: ChatGPT is the app you use, and the LLM is the engine under the hood doing the work. Then image 3, the name letter by letter: Large — trained on huge amounts of text, books, websites, even code, and that scale is what lets one model handle many tasks; Language — built for human language, reading, writing, summarizing, words in, words out; Model — the trained AI itself, which takes your input and predicts a likely output based on patterns it learned from data.
