Focus: the magic behind every AI you have used is math — two ideas from probability plus the loop that turns them into language. Open by saying the viewer already met these by name in How an LLM Works, and now gets to see each one work.

Where it began: in 1654 two French mathematicians, Blaise Pascal and Pierre de Fermat, traded letters about gambling, and that correspondence is where standard probability is usually dated from. Name them both aloud.

Standard probability: toss one coin, list the outcomes heads and tails, and walk the formula — ways it happens (1) over total outcomes (2) equals 50%. Then two coins: draw all four outcomes HH, HT, TH, TT and walk it again — 1 over 4 equals 25%. Draw both as clean tables.

Conditional probability: standard probability counts what you can see but cannot tell you how much to change your mind when new evidence arrives. Thomas Bayes — a minister, mathematician and philosopher — worked that out, and it is called Bayes' Theorem. Name him and name the theorem. Then run the update: someone peeks and says the first coin landed heads, so cross out every outcome where the first coin was tails, recount, and the probability moves from 25% to 50%. Show the crossing-out.

Autoregressive generation: start with the phone keyboard suggesting your next word — say that out loud as the way in. Autoregressive generation is that, except it does not stop after one word: after AI picks a word it uses that word to pick the next, then uses both to pick the third, so every prediction depends on every word before it.

Then tie the three together on one rainy afternoon: standard probability gives the 40% base rate from a hundred years of May 21sts; conditional probability updates it to 60% with the clue that humidity is 90%; autoregressive generation writes the forecast one word at a time — It, is, going, to — and then picks from rain 71%, pour 18%, stay 7%.

Close honestly, in the lesson's own words: the real math goes way past what is here — linear algebra moves the numbers, calculus tunes the model during training, and plenty of other math is in there too. But two ideas from probability plus the loop are the foundation. Then the close board: "Two ideas, plus the loop." over "Everything else is engineering."
