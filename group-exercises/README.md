## Discussion question format

Include discussion questions only when a lesson invites meaningful discussion.
Skip sets that mainly test recall or repeat discussions better suited to another
lesson. Five questions is a useful starting point, but use fewer when only two
or three are strong. Render approved sets inside `GroupExercise`, labeled
**Discussion Questions**, using `DiscussionQuestions`.
Probability & Prediction has four questions, How AI Learns, Transformer, and Document Trap have three,
and Embeddings and How AI Answers have two by David's explicit choice. Do not expand those sets
without his approval.
Prefer a short setup, a main question, and at most one optional follow-up.
Use the shared `context` field for one or two sentences describing a familiar
situation or small dilemma. Questions that do not need a setup can stand alone.
Never use em dashes in questions, follow-ups, introductions, or review drafts.
Hyphens are okay. Rewrite sentences with ordinary punctuation instead.
Setups should make the question concrete without explaining the lesson or
steering students toward an answer. Invite
experiences, judgments, competing perspectives, and tradeoffs. Avoid recall,
knowledge checks, or questions that simply lead students to repeat the lesson's
conclusion. Follow-ups should deepen the conversation rather than add a quiz.
Use the introduction: “Choose a question to discuss. Use the follow-ups if they
help the conversation.” Keep the main question bold and the follow-up smaller,
without a separate number. Preserve specific comparisons and tensions in the
main question rather than broadening it to a generic feelings question.
Use **Format A: Stepped follow-up** for the shared lesson component and public
discussion pages. Keep each item in one vertical reading flow: setup, bold main
question, then a smaller regular-weight follow-up with a green bent arrow and a
hanging indent. Leave 8px between setup and question and 12px before the follow-up.
Keep the large green serif numbers, thin row dividers, and white card inside a
mint frame. Retain this stacked layout on phones; do not split scenarios or
follow-ups into side columns. Decorative numbers and arrows are hidden from
assistive technology while the ordered list preserves the question count.
Keep the disclosure optional, collapsed by default, below the lesson's individual
TRY IT or LAB and before a Group Exercise when both exist.

Review one lesson at a time in course order, including existing question sets.
Propose questions in chat, incorporate David's feedback, and add the agreed
set before moving to the next lesson. Assess technical lessons individually;
do not force a discussion into every lesson. Skip Welcome, all Opener lessons,
and the entire Finish Smarter section.

## Activity instruction format

Activity-based lesson exercises use the shared `GroupActivityCard` through
`GroupExercise` or `StandaloneGroupExercise` in `index.html`. Keep the introduction
above the mint frame. Inside the white card, use a title with a divider, numbered
instruction rows with green serif numerals, and a tinted footer containing setup
details and the launch link. Supporting notes belong below the steps, without a
number. Footer details can include an existing duration, equipment, or round count;
do not invent a time estimate. On phones, the footer stacks above a full-width button.

Learn with AI keeps its prompt builder between the steps and the footer, with
**Open Study Mode** as its launch label. Discussion questions retain their separate
question format. See [Page components](../docs/components/page-components.md#groupexercise).

## Shared public directory

`../group-activities-data.js` is the directory used by both the splash-page
Group Activities dialog and the optional For Groups page under Finish Smarter. It includes all existing activities and
discussions in course order. Add new entries there with type, lesson, section,
description, and destination. Keep the lesson and section labels aligned with
`SECTION_META` and `SECTION_GROUPS` in `index.html`.

The discussions use this same data in both their lesson and
the public `group-session.html?id=...` view. The public study activity includes a
prompt builder. These pages require no course enrollment and do not change saved
course progress. `group-session.html` without an ID provides a full-page directory.

- [Where Do You Stand?](where-do-you-stand/where-do-you-stand.html) belongs to
  Where’s the Line? (`wherestheline`).
- [What’s Missing?](whats-missing/whats-missing.html) belongs to
  Critical Thinking (`critical`).

Run `node scripts/test-group-directory.cjs` from the repository root to check
coverage, shared question references, and local destinations.

## Shared assets and compatibility

- `what-is-ai/` contains Be the Last Person Standing, its 30 pictures, and their
  source and generation notes. The Fake Trap individual activity also reads the
  pictures from this folder. Keep one copy for both uses. The existing
  `fake-trap-try-*` filenames preserve the image-pair mapping.
- `does-ai-think/` and `in-your-hands/` contain the activity pages, pictures, and
  source notes for Spot What’s Wrong and Prompetition. The old
  `spot-whats-wrong/review.html` URL redirects to the updated review.
- The four original root HTML files are compatibility redirects, not editable
  activity sources. Edit the versions in the subfolders. Keep the redirects so
  existing bookmarks and previously generated previews continue to work.
- Standalone group exercises open in a new tab. Finish screens say “Close this
  tab to return to the course.” Keep replay and within-activity controls, but do
  not add course-return links or change the course’s saved navigation state.
- When nesting an activity, verify its icon, image, and launch paths.
