[0.00–3.32] Most people assume working with AI is a simple transaction.
[4.10–8.12] You type the perfect prompt, and the AI returns the perfect answer.
[8.80–9.94] But that's only the start.
[10.96–14.16] Think back to the best group project you've ever done in history class.
[14.86–17.58] Your group didn't start with the perfect finished project.
[18.16–21.96] You shared ideas, figured out what was missing, and improved it together.
[22.78–24.70] That's the same way you should work with AI.
[25.38–27.80] The group is smaller, just you and the AI,
[27.80–30.62] but the best result still takes a conversation.
[31.78–33.94] There are four distinct moves to this process.
[34.90–37.90] Think, learn, start, and iterate.
[38.60–39.94] The very first move is think.
[40.56–42.16] Use AI as a thought partner.
[42.88–45.74] Just like in that history project, you brainstorm ideas,
[46.12–50.40] ask questions, suggest next steps, and help one another decide what works.
[51.16–54.82] Bounce ideas back and forth, but remember that you make the final decisions.
[55.58–57.02] Here is how that looks in practice.
[57.58–61.24] You say, I'd like to earn money this summer by starting a small business.
[61.74–63.26] Can we talk through what might fit me?
[63.82–65.92] What are you already good at and what problems
[65.92–68.40] to people around you regularly need help solving?
[69.00–72.38] You reply, I'm good with dogs and teaching younger kids math.
[72.92–76.34] The AI answers, that gives us three realistic directions.
[76.82–79.70] Pet care, math tutoring, and a neighborhood errand service.
[80.20–83.58] Which sounds most interesting to you, and which would be easiest to start?
[84.34–85.74] The second move is learn.
[86.38–89.66] During that history group project, some members probably knew more
[89.66–91.38] about a specific subject than others.
[91.90–94.70] If a group member didn't understand a concept, they asked the group.
[95.46–96.98] Work with AI in the same way.
[97.56–101.60] When something does not make sense, use the conversation to understand something new.
[102.40–105.78] Ask follow-up questions, connect the idea to something you already know,
[106.10–107.74] and check whether you understand it.
[108.30–111.74] Then explain the idea back in your own words and ask what you missed.
[112.38–114.42] Let's test it on a business concept.
[115.06–118.76] You say, I might start a business this summer mowing lawns.
[119.08–121.60] What is profit and how do I calculate it?
[122.10–125.14] Explain it using simple numbers that apply to a lawn mowing business.
[125.94–131.04] The AI answers, profit is the money left after you subtract every cost from your sales.
[131.60–132.72] Your sales are $300.
[133.38–138.24] Your total costs are $150, leaving you with $150 in profit.
[138.84–142.04] The formula is sales minus costs equals profit.
[142.92–144.60] The third move is start.
[145.30–148.06] For the history project, the teacher gave your group a clear topic,
[148.34–149.98] like what caused the Civil War?
[150.48–154.22] You knew exactly where to begin, but real life isn't always that clear.
[155.08–158.64] What if you face a big decision or issue and don't even know where to start?
[159.16–161.96] In those moments, AI can help you find a place to begin.
[162.70–164.58] Simply ask it for the first question.
[165.18–168.08] For instance, you say, I need to start thinking about college,
[168.14–169.36] but I don't know where to begin.
[169.36–173.42] Ask me one question at a time to help me figure out what matters to me.
[173.76–174.88] Don't recommend a college yet.
[175.60–177.74] Do you already have a career or field in mind,
[178.12–180.84] or do you want college to help you explore different possibilities?
[181.90–183.44] The fourth move is iterate.
[184.08–186.24] That history group project lasted four weeks.
[186.66–189.06] At the end of week one, your project was only average.
[189.66–191.48] Each week, the group made it better and better.
[191.98–194.88] By the time you presented it to the class, it was a masterpiece.
[195.74–198.26] To iterate means to build something inversions,
[198.26–200.60] using each version to improve the next.
[201.40–203.40] Working with AI follows the exact same pattern.
[204.40–207.76] As you add details, correct weak spots, and improve the draft,
[208.26–211.64] the AI has better material to question, evaluate, and improve.
[212.56–214.66] Let's track a business plan across two rounds.
[215.22–219.06] In the early round, you say, give me ideas for a summer business.
[219.62–221.68] The AI answers, here are some ideas.
[222.42–226.24] Lawn care, pet sitting, tutoring, car washing, and selling crafts online.
[226.96–230.68] In the later round, you say, I'm considering a lawn cutting business.
[231.08–234.24] I'll serve homes within biking distance, use the customer's mower,
[234.64–237.38] work weekday afternoons, and charge by lawn size.
[237.98–240.08] Help me find weaknesses in the plan and improve it.
[240.76–243.38] The AI answers, let's pressure test it.
[243.74–245.00] How will you quote lawn sizes?
[245.48–246.42] What happens when it rains?
[247.26–249.50] The next level starts after the first answer.
[250.20–252.80] Think, learn, start, and iterate with AI.
