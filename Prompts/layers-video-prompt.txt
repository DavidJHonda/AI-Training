TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Open with the English-class hook and read the horse sentence once; leave a beat after “fell.” Explain First Read, More Reads, and Meaning Clicks: someone raced a horse past a barn, then the horse fell. Explain how repeated passes update meaning. Immediately bridge with “AI does something similar: it builds meaning through repeated updates.” Speak this before the human/AI distinction; a heading or later callback cannot replace it. Explain layers, attention and transformation, updated numbers passing to the next layer, and each pass building on the previous one. Define neural network once. The numbers board teaches the general process: numbers in, successive updates, final numbers out. Each row has many numbers; two are shown. Speak only first/final pairs, not middle values. Then explicitly move from that process to following one word in a real sentence. Read the complete cat sentence before Start. Explain all five stages, how the connection strengthens, continued updates beyond Layer 2, and the result. Return to the horse example to explain harder meaning, qualified scale (dozens, sometimes over a hundred), and why computing power and time limit added depth. Do not imply two numbers represent the entire word, call them probabilities, say ‘it’ is ‘cat,’ name models or give exact layer counts, narrate the AI Brain Break, or preview another lesson.

VOICE
Use the Markdown’s plain sentences and brief connectives. Read ‘it’ and ‘cat’ as words, never spelled letters. Do not say AI thinks, understands, or has a brain. Avoid neurons, weights, parameters, vectors, embeddings, algorithm, deep learning, and garden path. Never ask viewers to pause or guess; supply answers.
