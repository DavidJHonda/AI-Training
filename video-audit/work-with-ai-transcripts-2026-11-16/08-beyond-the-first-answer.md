# 8. Beyond the First Answer

`course-assets/beyond-the-first-answer/beyond-the-first-answer.mp4` · 3:49 · 666 words

**[0:00]** Most people assume working with AI is a simple transaction. You type the perfect prompt, and the AI returns the perfect answer. But that's only the start.

**[0:10]** Think back to the best group project you've ever done in history class. Your group didn't start with the perfect, finished project. You shared ideas, figured out what was missing, and improved it together. That's the same way you should work with AI. The group is smaller, just you and the AI, but the best result still takes a conversation.

**[0:31]** There are four distinct moves to this process. First, thought partner. Second, learn with AI. Third, find a place to start. And fourth, iterate with AI.

**[0:45]** So this brings us to our first move, thought partner. You're just exchanging questions and bouncing ideas around to find a fitting summer business. And keep in mind, the student makes the final decisions. Let's look at the chat. You say, I'd like to earn money this summer by starting a small business. Can we talk through what might fit me? AI answers, what are you already good at? And what problems do people around you regularly need help solving?

**[1:10]** Then you reply, I'm good with dogs and teaching younger kids math. The AI follows up, that gives us three realistic directions, pet care, math tutoring, and a neighborhood errand service. Which sounds most interesting to you and which would be easiest to start? A good conversation asks about you before it answers.

**[1:28]** The second move is learn with AI. When something feels confusing, you can ask the tool to explain it. Ask followup questions, connect new ideas to things you already know, check your understanding, and then explain it back in your own words. You ask, I might start a business this summer mowing lawns. What is profit and how do I calculate it? Explain it using simple numbers that apply to a lawn mowing business. And here is the AI's response, complete with a visual equation. Profit is the money left after you subtract every cost from your sales. For example, your sales are $300. Your total costs are $150, leaving you with $150 in profit. The formula is sales minus costs equals profit. Ask AI to explain the idea using a situation you know.

**[2:14]** Moving right along, our third move is find a place to start.

**[2:17]** This approach is absolutely perfect for when you face a big decision and have literally no idea how to begin. The goal here is to take a massive overwhelming decision like choosing a path forward for college and turn it into a single initial question.

**[2:32]** Let's look at the prompt. You say, I need to start thinking about college but I don't know where to begin. Ask me one question at a time to help me figure out what matters to me. Don't recommend a college yet. The AI answers, do you already have a career or field in mind or do you want college to help you explore different possibilities? When you don't know where to begin, ask AI for the first question. Iterate with AI.

**[2:55]** To iterate means to build something in versions, using each version to improve the next. We can clearly contrast a vague early request with a sharp, detailed business plan to see exactly how this iteration works in practice. Let's look at the early round first. You say, give me ideas for a summer business. The AI replies, here are some ideas. Lawn care, pet sitting, tutoring, car washing, and selling crafts online. Now look at the later round. You prompt, I'm considering a lawn cutting business. I'll serve homes within biking distance, use the customer's mower, work weekday afternoons and charge by lawn size. Help me find weaknesses in the plan and improve it. The AI responds, let's pressure test it. How will you quote lawn sizes? What happens when it rains? The next level starts after the first answer.

**[3:42]** Explore ideas, build understanding and improve together.
