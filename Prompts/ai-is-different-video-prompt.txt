Focus: AI is built on a different foundation — patterns, not rules — and every difference follows from that.

Open on the foundation. Normal software is written by a programmer line by line, and its core is rules — most commonly IF-THEN-ELSE. Walk the lesson's example aloud: IF the password matches, THEN open the app, ELSE show "Password doesn't match." Then the turn: AI is built on patterns, not rules.

Then the cooking analogy. Normal software is a robot cooking from a cookbook: someone wrote every step, so it makes the same dish every time. AI is a chef: no cookbook, it cooked thousands of dishes and learned the patterns, so it can handle one it has never made. Same kitchen, different way to dinner.

Then the PS5 question. Ask "what's the best game for my new PS5?" Normal software returns the same preset list no matter who asks — ask again, same list. AI answers Spider-Man 2, then NHL 26, then God of War Ragnarok. Land the split: fixed rules hand back the same list; patterns build a fresh answer every time, shaped to your question.

Then the two side-by-side boards, one after the other. Hold each and walk its three rows — what it needs, so how it acts, superpower — speaking the reason written under each, in the board's own words. Never read the headings and move on; the contrast between the boards IS the lesson. Ground it in how this lesson was written: messy legal-pad notes, scratched out and arrowed around, that Word or Excel could not take and AI could.

Then the right tool for the job: a superpower isn't the right tool. When a job is exact and has to stay consistent — a GPA, a password — normal software wins. AI earns the messy, open-ended jobs no one could write a rule for. The skill isn't reaching for AI every time; it's knowing which kind of job you have.

Then AI's Kryptonite. Trained behavior is harder to predict, inspect and lock down than written rules, and nobody can fully predict what it will do — not the engineers, not the researchers, not the company that ships it. Then the stories board, held while the narration walks its three cards, speaking the reason under each. Then the mechanism, plainly: AI is being steered, not authored. What's inside isn't rules or even readable patterns, it's millions of learned numbers — which is exactly why you can't open it up and read it.

Then guardrails: companies can't rewrite the learned patterns, so they wrap a safety layer around the model. It's called a guardrail, it blocks, redirects or limits specific behaviors, and none are perfect. When an app refuses something dangerous, that's the guardrail talking, not the AI making a call of its own.

Close board: "Pick the tool that fits the job." over "Patterns handle the mess. Rules stay consistent."
