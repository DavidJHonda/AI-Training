Proposed illustration

Use the same school team and court through three clear states: a familiar passing lane, a defender closing that lane, and the captain spotting an open teammate and choosing a different pass. Retain a small, simple court inset only where it helps show which route changed.

  1. 0:00–0:10: establish the captain, teammates and familiar route in a school gym.
  2. 0:10–0:19: show the defender blocking that route. Keep court positions consistent.
  3. 0:19–0:30.37: emphasize the open alternative on “curious,” then the changed choice on “flexible.” Return to the existing update scene as the narration transfers the analogy to AI.

Learning benefit: Separates looking for another option from actually abandoning the old play. The basketball analogy becomes concrete before the video transfers it to changing AI workflows.

Keep it only if: At normal speed, the learner can distinguish discovering an alternative from switching to it, and the photos and route inset agree about player positions.

Narration and proposed treatment
First, you have to be curious enough to actively look for a better play. Second, you must be flexible enough to stop relying on the old one, even if you know it by heart.

Three or four coherent photographic states with minimal separately rendered route overlays; avoid a single long hold or a busy sports montage. Use “Curious: look for another play” and “Flexible: change the play” only on their matching cues. Do not add a new score, a guaranteed-success badge, or a made basket. The existing exit is frame 911 (0:30.37); preserve that transition and duration.

Course boards: Keep Stay Curious and Be Flexible, all existing interface cutaways about updates, newsletters, familiar work and comparison, the repaired final process diagram, and the close. This proposal touches only the opening analogy.
