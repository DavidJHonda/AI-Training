**[0:00]** You need to start your next history project, but you haven't picked a topic. You look around the room for inspiration. Ask your desk, give me 10 ideas for my next history project.

**[0:11]** Nothing. It's a desk.

**[0:14]** Ask a friend, and they can help you brainstorm.

**[0:17]** Ask AI, and it can give you a list in seconds. AI is software built to do things that used to take a human brain. It can understand a question, summarize a long document or translate a conversation. That doesn't mean it actually thinks like your friend or that all 10 of those history ideas will be good. You simply have a tool that can help with cognitive tasks that once required a person.

**[0:42]** Different AI systems do different jobs. Let's look at two kinds you already use. This graphic breaks down these tools into two main categories. First is recommendation AI. Its job is to choose from what already exists. It works by ranking the available options and selecting the likely best match for you. You see this every day when you get a suggested Netflix show, a Spotify song, or a maps route. Then there is generative AI. Its job is to make something that didn't exist. The engine behind this creation process is something called a prompt. That's simply the specific question or set of instructions you type into the system. This can result in a brand new email, an essay, an image, a website, a song, or a video.

**[1:29]** AI left the realm of science fiction years ago to become part of your daily routine. It operates the apps on your phone and powers the tools your first job will hand you on day one. Most of what it does today, it will do better tomorrow.

**[1:43]** This graphic shows a breakdown of exactly where AI already lives in your daily routine. First, it recommends media by predicting what you might like next. You interact with this on Spotify, Netflix, and TikTok.

**[1:57]** Second, navigation tools predict traffic and your arrival time on apps like Google Maps and Waze.

**[2:03]** Third, face recognition validates your identity, checking whether a face matches you for phone unlocks and photo tagging.

**[2:10]** Fourth, voice assistants turn your speech into words when you talk to Siri, Alexa, or Hey Google. Finally, chatbots carry on direct conversations with you through programs like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.

**[2:24]** Looking at this complete picture shows us something clear. AI was already part of your day before chatbots arrived.
