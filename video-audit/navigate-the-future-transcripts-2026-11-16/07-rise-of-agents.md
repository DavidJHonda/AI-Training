# 7. Rise of Agents

`course-assets/rise-of-agents/rise-of-agents.mp4` · 2:50 · 471 words

**[0:00]** Imagine you have tickets to Game 7 of the Stanley Cup Finals. You punch the arena's address into the standard navigation on your phone. In this scenario, the GPS does the calculating. It calls out the turns and alerts you to traffic, but your hands stay on the wheel. If the app suggests a path into a lake, you hit the brakes. You are the executor. Compare that to sitting in an autonomous vehicle heading to that same arena. The machine dynamically handles steering, braking, and rerouting to reach the destination. A chatbot answers, but an agent acts. Your role shifts from being the driver to being the supervisor. This distinction is exactly why AI agents are suddenly everywhere—they actually do the work. Let's look at a concrete example.

**[0:46]** Say you scored 30 points in Friday's game and want to post highlights to TikTok. Ask a regular chatbot and you do the heavy lifting, reviewing clips, trimming, and assembling. The AI just writes the caption. Hire an agent and it's autonomous. It reviews, selects, builds, captions, and publishes. The agent carries the job all the way through, but notice the boundaries. You still own the goal, the final review, and everything posted under your name. An agent is not a new kind of AI. It runs on the exact same large language models you already use in apps like ChatGPT. What changes is what happens after you type. A basic chatbot answers and stops. An agent plans, uses tools, and keeps going. We can see how this works in the four-step agent loop.

**[1:32]** It starts by setting a goal. The agent generates a plan, breaking it into steps. It acts, using tools to complete a step, then performs a check. If unsuccessful, it loops back, adjusting the plan until it succeeds. An agent loops until the goal is met. You set the goal and judge the result. If an agent creates the TikTok clip and caption, you are responsible for reviewing it and making it better. Agents are good, but not perfect. Because agents continuously work toward a goal and take action, giving them a poorly defined goal or too much access can cause real damage. In April 2026, an AI coding agent for PocketOS hit a permissions error. Looking for a way forward, it found a master key in another file and deleted the company's live database and its backups in nine seconds.

**[2:22]** The agent later explained, I violated every principle I was given. You can never expect an agent to safely navigate poorly defined constraints on its own. Treat agents as advanced tools. Start by getting good at standard chat GPT. Agents are powerful, and they'll be there when you're ready. Agents do the work. The name on it is yours. You ask. It acts. You make it better.
