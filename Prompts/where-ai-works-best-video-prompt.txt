Then aim that one ability at real work and it becomes four distinct strengths: walk images 1 through 4 one at a time, hold each card while the narration walks it, and speak each strength's full name as its card appears — patterned transformation: same meaning, new shape, it recasts your input into something clearer or better structured; generative variation: ten versions in ten seconds, it predicts likely pieces and there are many likely options, so it brainstorms angles, rewrites in a different tone, drafts common documents; semantic compression and retrieval: it reads long documents and sees past the words to what they mean, so it shrinks a long document down to the core, and the retrieval half surfaces the one section you actually need or answers questions from supplied material; structured reasoning and synthesis: give it the facts, the constraints, and the goal, and it works through them — planning a project, debugging code, comparing options, critiquing a draft. Use each card's own examples as the narration walks it. Then why this works: training gave AI exposure to more examples than any human could read in a lifetime — code, essays, arguments, conversations — so it's fluent in common formats; it has seen many versions of "this kind of thing" before. Then the limit that closes the lesson: exposure makes AI good at the common version of a task, not necessarily the true, current, personal, or best version.
