Open on the problem the lesson answers: AI makes mistakes, and it uses the same confident voice whether the facts are right or wrong. So what do you do with an answer that might be wrong? One option is to ignore AI — and nobody this far into the course is doing that, because AI is too powerful to leave on the shelf. Name the course: to Be Smarter Than the Tool, you need the skills to evaluate the results.

Then the Petronoski example, given real time; it is the sharpest thing in the lesson. "Maria Petronoski won 3 gold medals at the 1932 Olympics in Athens" sounds perfectly fine. Then the rule — the more specific the detail, the more skeptical you should be — and the limit: when a topic is beyond what you know, "sounds fine" isn't validation.

What kind of task was this: fact work needs more evaluation, while a first draft or brainstorm just gets checked against what you asked for.
