9.900–117.967 hanoi [source 1]
The best way to see it is a true story about rats. In 1902, the city of Hanoi was rapidly modernizing. The colonial government laid down miles of new state -of -the -art sewer pipes. Unfortunately, those dark, protected tunnels provided the perfect breeding ground for a massive rat infestation. City officials devised what looked like a mathematically flawless solution. They created a bounty system, offering citizens a small cash payment for every severed rat tail handed in. Initially, the metrics looked phenomenal. Tales poured into government offices by the thousands on a daily basis. But shortly after the program launched, citizens started noticing healthy, completely tailless rats screwing around the city streets. The math had a biological loophole. A rat can survive perfectly fine without its tail. And more importantly, it can keep making more rat babies. The rat hunters figured out that killing the animal destroyed their own supply chain. So, they caught the rats, clipped the tails to collect the cash, and released them back into the sewers to breed. A dead rat pays once. A live rat pays forever. Some local entrepreneurs realized hunting wild rats was too much work, so they bypassed the sewers entirely and set up dedicated rat breeding farms. The government eventually caught onto the scam and completely scrapped the bounty program. They were left with a literal mountain of severed tails in their offices, but the actual living rat population in Hanoi was significantly higher than before the bounty started. But the real goal was fewer rats, and the program encouraged people to keep rats alive. Hanoi's plan was fewer rats. The unexpected result was more rats.
