## AVOID TRAPS

# Fake Trap

Here’s the strange thing about this trap: you already know it. AI can fake a voice, a face, a video, a screenshot, cheaply and convincingly, and it’s only getting better. So why do smart people still get fooled?

Because knowing fakes exist isn’t a skill. It doesn’t tell you what to do in the ten seconds after a clip hits your feed.

### Board 1: Same Clip. Two Eras.

**Image file:** `fake-trap-comparison-faceless.jpg`

![Same Clip. Two Eras.](fake-trap-comparison-faceless.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

A friend sends a video of your principal announcing that school is closed next week.

**Before AI**

Does it look real? You study the face, voice, and hallway. Everything looks right.

**Your call:** I’d believe it.

**The AI Era**

Where is it from? You ignore the pixels and check the trail. You find no current closure notice on the school website.

**Your call:** I’d wait for the school to confirm it.

Appearance can mislead. The source trail can be checked.

The Fake Trap is believing it because it looks real. And there’s the other side: dismissing the truth because it could be a fake.

## Friendly or Harmful

A friend fakes a picture of your hockey buddies hoisting the Stanley Cup. Everyone knows it is fake, and everyone is in on the joke. No harm.

### Board 2: Why People Make Harmful Fakes.

**Image file:** `fake-trap-reasons.jpg`

![Why People Make Harmful Fakes.](fake-trap-reasons.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

Money: outrage gets clicks, and clicks pay.

Power: change what people believe and you change how they vote, protest, and spend.

Fame: a viral clip means followers. It does not have to be true to travel.

Cruelty: some fakes exist to humiliate one person, especially at school.

## So what do you do?

Fakes can travel quickly when they spike your emotions: outrage, fear, excitement, or hope. A strong feeling is your cue to stop and run three checks before you react, share, or believe.

If you try an AI detector, treat its verdict as a clue, never a ruling. Those tools make mistakes.

### Board 3: Three Ways to Check a Clip.

**Image file:** `fake-trap-checks.jpg`

![Three Ways to Check a Clip.](fake-trap-checks.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

Source: who posted it? Do they have a reason and a way to know?

Context: When was this recorded? What did the speaker say before and after this part?

Corroboration: can an independent source confirm the same event or claim?

Go back to the principal’s clip. Look for the full announcement on the school website and check which dates it covers. If the dates are unclear, ask the school office using the contact information you already have. Until the school confirms next week’s closure, the claim is unverified.

Verify somewhere the sender does not control.

## Fakes about you

If someone posts a fake about you or makes an account pretending to be you, tell an adult you trust. If it’s safe, write down the username, link, and date so you can report it together.

If someone makes or shares a fake nude or sexually explicit image of anyone under 18, don’t screenshot, download, or forward it, even to make a report. Ask a trusted adult to help you report it through the platform. NCMEC’s Take It Down and CyberTipline can also help.

## Closing Message

Seeing or hearing isn’t proof anymore.

Check the source, not the pixels.
