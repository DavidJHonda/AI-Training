# AI is Different

Today’s AI behaves differently from traditional software. If you expect it to act like the software you’re used to, its behavior can be confusing.

Two differences help explain why. First, AI learns patterns from examples rather than relying only on rules written by programmers. Second, it can work with “unstructured data,” such as everyday language, images, and audio, with far greater flexibility than traditional software. These differences make AI more capable in many situations, but also less predictable.

## Rules-Based Software

You’re signing into Spotify. You enter the wrong password. Should the app open because you were close? Or because your spelling was creative?

No. You want it to follow a rule: **let me in only if the password matches.**

Non-AI software is based on instructions like this that people wrote. A calculator follows instructions for mathematical operations. A spreadsheet applies formulas to the information you enter.

## Rules vs. Patterns

Traditional software follows instructions written by programmers.

AI learns patterns from examples during training and uses those patterns to answer you.

People still write the code that trains and runs AI, but they don’t write each answer.

AI is like a chef who learned from preparing many different dishes. The chef recognizes patterns: which ingredients work together, how cooking methods affect food, and what substitutions might work.

The chef doesn’t need a recipe.

Learned patterns. Different dishes.

## Structured vs. Unstructured Data

Traditional software usually needs information entered in a specific structure.

Think of a spreadsheet, with rows and columns. That’s **structured data**.

AI can work with **unstructured data**. You don’t need to organize it into specific rows and columns. Give it what you have, and it can help make sense of it.

## How AI Interprets the Notes

AI can do this because, during training, it learned patterns in handwriting, language, and visual clues like arrows and crossed-out words.

It uses those patterns to help interpret your notes, even when they aren’t neatly organized.

## The downside

Using learned patterns makes AI flexible, but its response can be harder to predict and inspect than a written rule like the password check.

It might miss a crossed-out song.

It might look great, but still have mistakes.
