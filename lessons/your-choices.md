# Know the App

Taking a picture on your phone is easy. Open the camera app, point, and tap. For most pictures, that’s all you need.

But sometimes you need something different. You want a family picture, but it’s dark outside. Better turn on the flash. Or you’re sitting high in the stands at a hockey game and want a picture of your favorite player. Better zoom in.

You don’t change these settings for every picture. But knowing when to use them can help you get the picture you want.

AI works much the same way.
Depending on the app and your subscription, you may have three other choices: the model, thinking, and research.
You won’t need to change them every time.
But knowing what they do helps you get a better answer.

## 1-The Model

When you use an AI app like ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude, an LLM powers its answers. Think of the LLM as the engine inside the app.

When an app lets you choose a “model,” you’re choosing which LLM to use.
Different models have different capabilities.
Knowing which model to choose helps you get better answers.

### Board 1: Which Model?

**Image file:** `your-choices-choose-tool.jpg`

![Which Model?](your-choices-choose-tool.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

Which model?

The Everyday Model is for quick questions and simple tasks.

For example, ask: “Suggest a funny movie to watch tonight.”

The More Capable Model is for more demanding tasks with details to work through.

For example, ask: “Help me plan a summer business, including costs, pricing, and how many customers I’ll need.”

Start with the default.
Try a more capable model when needed.

## 2-Thinking

Choosing a model changes which LLM you use.
Thinking changes how much time AI spends working through your task.

Think about it like this. For your first English exam, you have to write an essay about the book you read over the summer. Before you start writing, you decide what you want to say, which examples support your points, and how to organize them.

Thinking mode serves a similar purpose.
AI isn’t thinking the way you do, but it spends more time working through the task before giving you an answer.

### Board 2: How Much Thinking?

**Image file:** `your-choices-thinking.jpg`

![How Much Thinking?](your-choices-thinking.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

How much thinking?

With Less Thinking, AI spends less time working through your task. It is useful for less complicated and routine work.

For example, ask: “Turn these notes from our group meeting into a clear checklist.”

With More Thinking, AI spends more time working through steps, weighing options, and checking its answer. It is useful for tasks that require analysis like coding, planning, and problems with steps.

For example, ask: “Help us divide this project among four people. We have one week, different schedules, and only two people know how to edit video.”

Give AI more time when the task needs more working through.

## 3-Research

There’s one more option: Research.

You already know when you need to do some research. Stop at In-N-Out for a cheeseburger, and you know enough to place your order.

Buying your first house is different. You’d want to compare prices, learn about the neighborhoods, and check how far each house is from work. It’s a bigger decision, and you need more information before making it.

AI’s research mode helps with that kind of work. It gathers information from different websites and brings it together so you can compare your options.

Regular chat can look things up, too.
Research is useful when you want a fuller investigation across several sources.

### Board 3: How Much Research?

**Image file:** `your-choices-research.jpg`

![How Much Research?](your-choices-research.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

How much research?

Use Regular Chat to get an answer to a question.

For example, ask: “I live in Dallas. How long would it take me to drive to Texas A&M in College Station versus UT Austin?”

Use Deep Research to get information from many websites, compare what they say, and build a report that shows sources.

For example, ask: “I want to study physics and am considering Texas A&M and UT Austin. Compare their physics programs, undergraduate research opportunities, admissions requirements, and costs. Show your sources.”

Use deeper research when you need to compare information across sources.

## Closing Message

Model. Thinking. Research.

Use them when the work demands more.
