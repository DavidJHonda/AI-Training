# What’s an LLM?

If you’ve used AI, you’ve probably used an app like ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini. The app is what you use. Under the hood, a Large Language Model, or LLM for short, does the work.

### Board 1: What’s an LLM?

**Image file:** `whats-an-llm-llm.jpg`

![What’s an LLM?](whats-an-llm-llm.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

Large: trained on huge amounts of text and code. Language: it reads, writes, summarizes, translates, and explains. Model: it predicts likely output from learned patterns.

ChatGPT is the app. The LLM is the engine.

## Learning Patterns

Before you use it, the model goes through training, learning patterns from enormous amounts of text and other data.

Here’s one you picked up as a child. Which word comes next?

### Board 2: Patterns AI Learns

**Image file:** `whats-an-llm-patterns.jpg`

![Patterns AI Learns](whats-an-llm-patterns.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

One familiar pattern: peanut butter and blank leads to jelly. You knew it. So does AI.

Patterns are everywhere: twinkle, twinkle, little blank leads to star. Once upon a blank leads to time. Better late than blank leads to never.

AI learns patterns by working through billions of examples.

## Beyond Familiar Phrases

These are easy patterns you already know. AI also learns patterns in how people explain ideas, ask questions, solve problems, and even misspell words.

## Building an Answer

When you ask a question, the model uses those learned patterns and the words already there to work out what could come next. It chooses a likely next word, adds it, and repeats.

The words already there change what might come next.

“Peanut butter and…” might lead to “jelly.” “Peanut butter and banana…” might lead to “sandwich.”

Your phone does something similar when you write a text: it suggests a word, you tap it, and it suggests the next. An LLM keeps going to build an answer.

### Board 3: One Word at a Time

**Image file:** `whats-an-llm-one-word-at-a-time.jpg`

![One Word at a Time](whats-an-llm-one-word-at-a-time.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

One word at a time. “I want to buy peanut butter and” leads to jelly. “I want to buy peanut butter and jelly” leads to for. “I want to buy peanut butter and jelly for” leads to lunch.

Add a word. Use the updated sentence. Predict again.

## The Growing Answer

Each added word becomes part of what the model uses for its next prediction.

More than one word can fit, so the same starting text can lead to different answers.
