# How AI Works: Key Facts

AI tokens are arcade coins. Every time you ask a question, a tiny robot drops one into a machine. Short questions take one coin. Long questions take a whole bucket, and the poor robot has to drag it across the room. The only thing slowing AI down is a small robot hauling change.

Training an AI means making it run laps around a data center. The more laps it runs, the smarter it gets. Skip the laps, and it gets tired halfway through a homework question and lies down. Intelligence, it turns out, is mostly cardio.

AI adds layers when it gets cold. Data centers are freezing, so a bigger model needs a bigger jacket. A hundred-layer model has a very crowded closet: scarves, mittens, one enormous parka. Making AI smarter is mostly finding more shelf space.

AI reads your mind through your cell phone. That is how its answers sound so personal. If it gets an answer wrong, the phone didn’t hear you. Try thinking louder.

Gemini was originally called Lemon Pie. Google’s engineers worked late, and their favorite midnight snack was, you guessed it, lemon pie. People kept asking if it came with ice cream, so at the last minute they changed the name to Gemini. It rhymes with Lemon Pie, so nobody would notice the switch. Nobody did.

ChatGPT used to be called YellGPT, because earlier AI was hard of hearing. Everyone had to shout. Once engineers fixed its hearing, the megaphones went away, and everyone could stop shouting and just chat.

Gemini and ChatGPT run on the Transformer, but Claude runs on a special type of pasta called Claude-A-Roni. For difficult questions, simply add more sauce.
