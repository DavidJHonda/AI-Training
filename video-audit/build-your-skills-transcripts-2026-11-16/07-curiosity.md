# 7. Curiosity

`course-assets/curiosity/curiosity.mp4` · 3:03 · 464 words

**[0:00]** Imagine you are the captain of your school's basketball team. You have a play that has worked beautifully all season. But suddenly, another team figures out how to shut it down. Before you find a better play, you need to be curious. You have to diagnose the failure. What exactly is the opponent doing differently? Where is the new opening? What could you learn by watching that play again? Taking an active interest, asking questions, and exploring possibilities reaches far beyond the basketball court. It helps you keep learning throughout your entire life. There's always more to discover.

**[0:35]** In this first panel, curiosity acts as your armor. Influencers, advertisers, and algorithms are constantly trying to shape what you think. When you encounter persuasive media, ask defensive questions. Who wants me to believe this? What is the evidence? And what crucial information is missing? Over on the right, curiosity kills boredom. A good question makes ordinary things interesting. Ask how a specific system works, why people choose to do it that particular way, and consider what could be done differently.

**[1:08]** Curiosity helps you grow by changing how you handle feedback. You will receive critical feedback throughout your life. Instead of shutting down, ask active questions. Find out exactly what worked, pinpoint what could improve, and ask for specific example so you can extract actionable steps. Finally, as this last panel explains, you can learn as you live. It is perfectly acceptable if you do not have your life figured out today. The key is to stay engaged, try new things, meet new people, and pay close attention to what genuinely captures your interest. Let the things you discover organically map out your next best step. Today, the rapid evolution of artificial intelligence is the ultimate unknown. New models, tools, and workflows keep appearing almost daily.

**[1:56]** You do not need to chase every single new feature. You only need to stay curious about the changes that could actually matter to you. Use this four-part framework to stay AI curious. The first habit is to use AI regularly, especially outside of your required schoolwork. This baseline familiarity makes it much easier to notice when an interface suddenly gains new abilities. Next, you should check what changed. Look out for update notices, new buttons, or model names. Constant monitoring isn't necessary. Checking occasionally gives you the information you need. To avoid feeling overwhelmed, follow one reliable source. Pick a single, trustworthy newsletter, educator, or official product page. Calling your inputs down to one curated feed provides enough information to stay informed.

**[2:44]** Finally, compare with others. Ask your friends, teachers, or club members what they have tried recently. Different people naturally stumble upon different tools and features. Curiosity keeps you growing. Keep asking questions. Keep learning as you live.
