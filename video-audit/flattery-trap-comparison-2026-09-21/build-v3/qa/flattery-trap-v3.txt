 173.04- 179.04  By giving the AI a clearer job and a standard to measure against, we prevent it from simply agreeing with us.

 182.52- 187.58  If you say I think this plan is good, thoughts, you hand the AI your opinion to echo.
 188.24- 190.08  Instead, hold back your verdict.

 195.68- 199.92  A neutral question gives the AI less of your own confidence to reflect back at you.
 200.72- 202.88  The second move is ask for the gaps.
 203.64- 209.66  Asking is my essay good is a broad request for approval, and a cheerful yes doesn't help you revise.
 210.54- 215.78  Ask specifically, what's weak, what's missing, and what would someone who disagrees say.
 215.78- 219.08  Now you have given the AI specific targets to locate.
 220.18- 222.28  The third move is use a rubric.
 222.84- 228.02  If you just say grade my essay, the AI might use criteria completely different from your teachers.
 228.72- 234.44  Instead, tell it, grade it against this rubric, quote the evidence, and show how to move up one level.

 239.82- 241.76  but the response is still just a guide.
 241.76- 247.18  It is something to check against your work, not an official guarantee of accuracy or a final grade.
 248.10- 250.44  The fourth move is argue the other side.
 250.96- 255.62  Asking don't you agree invites the system to side with the position you just shared.
 256.32- 262.52  Instead, instruct it to give me the three strongest counter arguments and explain why someone might hold them.

 276.20- 282.42  Each of these moves works because you are replacing a request for validation with a concrete standard for the AI to meet.
 283.22- 285.36  The fifth move is set a standing instruction.
