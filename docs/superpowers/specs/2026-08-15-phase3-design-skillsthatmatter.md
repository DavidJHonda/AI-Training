**Date:** 2026-08-15 · Phase 3 of the Finish Smarter merge

---

## Thesis

**When producing the first version costs nothing, the value moves to the person who decides what
gets made, catches when it goes strange, picks which version ships, and asks whether it should have
been made at all.**

---

## Arc position

- **Before:** Creative Thinking (`creativethinking`) — closes on "AI does the task. You bring the
  angle," then its "Same Problem, Different Thinking" TRY IT.
- **After:** Be Curious (`becurious`) — opens on the reps argument (nobody has a ten-year head
  start) and closes with "Curiosity or Outsourcing?"
- **Beat:** roundup lesson for the opener's `SKILLS AI WON'T REPLACE` group, which currently lists
  only Creative Thinking. This lesson is the second question in that group. Suggested opener entry
  (Phase 4 wiring, not this doc's job): `{ question: "What else stays mine when the tool does the
  producing?", lessonId: "skillsthatmatter" }`.

**Opening bridge intent.** The transition sits in the first sentence of this lesson, per the
house rule, and names no lesson title. It picks up the angle the previous lesson just finished
teaching and widens: an angle is one thing the tool can't hand you, and it isn't the only one.

**Two collision guards, both load-bearing on the copy:**

1. Creative Thinking already runs "everyone in the room has the same AI" **twice** in its own copy.
   This lesson must not open on that line. It opens on the shelf-life of production instead.
2. Be Curious owns curiosity, reps, and chasing the new thing. The adaptability beat here is the
   *opposite motion*: dropping what you were good at. Written so the two never say the same thing
   (see the boundary note in Beat E).

---

## Structure

Spine: **one job → any job → any year → everyone else.** The aperture widens beat by beat. That is
what makes this a lesson and not a drawer of cards. Each beat is a wider circle of the same
question, "what part of this doesn't get typed for you."

### Beat A — Opener (prose, no kicker)

`HUMANEDGE_SURVIVORS: opening production-to-direction thesis`, adapted. The middle clause of the
original ("deciding what to ask, judging whether the answer is good, catching what's wrong...") is
**cut**: it enumerates five of the CUT cards and pre-announces the four moves. Paragraph 2 is
adapted from "You've been building these moves all course" to admit that two of them haven't come
up yet, which is what earns the new material downstream.

Proposed copy:

> An angle is one thing the tool can't hand you. It isn't the only one, and the full list is shorter
> than the hype makes it sound.

> AI can produce a first draft of almost anything in seconds. That changes what makes a person
> valuable. Producing the first version used to be most of the job. Now it's the cheapest part of
> it. The bottleneck moves from production to direction.

> You've been building most of these moves all course. A couple haven't come up yet. What's left is
> knowing which one to bring in at which moment.

### Beat B — "Real problems don't arrive as prompts" (SectionKicker + prose + one demonstration box)

Carries `HUMANEDGE_SURVIVORS: SKILLS card "Break down messy problems"` and
`HUMANEDGE_SURVIVORS: SKILLS card "Taste, judgment, prioritization"`. **Adaptation: neither survives
as a card.** Two cards is a drawer, and a card restates. Both are demonstrated instead, in one
worked artifact, then landed in a single prose line each. This is the box that demonstrates.

Kicker: `REAL PROBLEMS DON'T ARRIVE AS PROMPTS`

> A prompt is a clean question. Almost nothing that actually lands on you is clean.

Demonstration box (ShowcaseBox, three labeled zones; headline "One messy problem, cut into three"):

- **WHAT LANDS ON YOU** — "Club attendance is down. Two officers think it's the meeting time, one
  thinks nobody outside the group chat knows the club exists, and there's one meeting left before
  break."
- **WHAT AI CAN ACTUALLY TAKE** (once you've cut it up) — "Draft a five-question survey that tells
  us which of those three it is." / "Give me ten meeting formats that work with fifteen people and
  no budget." / "Turn our best three into a poster and a group-chat message."
- **WHAT NEVER GOES IN THE BOX** — "Deciding this is a why-won't-they-come problem and not a
  what-do-we-do-when-they-get-there problem. And picking one format out of ten, knowing which one
  this group will actually show up for."

Landing prose (the two survivor cards, in-voice, after the demonstration):

> Notice the two places you did the work. You cut a tangled situation into pieces small enough to
> hand over, which is the part AI won't do for you, because it can't see the tangle. Then you
> picked. AI can hand you ten options in four seconds. It has no idea which one fits your people.

### Beat C — The four moves (SectionKicker + prose + EDGE_CARDS box)

`HUMANEDGE_SURVIVORS: EDGE_CARDS "Four moves AI can't own"` — **all four, verbatim names and
bodies**, box kicker verbatim (`FOUR MOVES AI CAN'T OWN FOR YOU`). Load-bearing: Make Your Move
cites these four by name.

Framing prose above the box (new, one paragraph, so the box isn't dropped cold):

> Those two run inside one job. Four more run across all of them, and they're worth knowing by
> name. Naming the move that's missing is how you fix a bad answer, instead of just sitting there
> feeling like something's off.

Cards (verbatim from the audit):

1. 🎯 **Frame the real problem** — "AI is much more useful when the human knows what needs to be
   solved, who it is for, and what a good answer would look like."
2. 🧠 **Make sense of weird behavior** — "When you know that AI works through patterns, probability,
   context, and prediction, its weird behavior starts to make sense."
3. 🔍 **Check what matters** — "AI can sound confident when it is wrong. The better it sounds, the
   more important it becomes to know what needs verification."
4. 🧭 **Make the human call** — "AI can generate options. It cannot own your judgment, your values,
   your reputation, or the consequences of what you choose."

**Dedupe note (deliberate, not an oversight):** "Check what matters" is adjacent to the CUT card
"Verify what matters," and the actual teaching of verification lives in Evaluate the Results. Here
the four are a **naming vocabulary for diagnosis**, not a re-teach: the body copy stays one line
each, no verification tactics, no dig-deeper apparatus, and the capstone asks the student to *name*
the missing move rather than to *perform* the check. If that line ever thickens into instruction,
it has crossed into forbidden territory.

### Beat D — Where the shelf life is (SectionKicker + prose) — NEW

David's addition 2(b): resilience and ambiguity tolerance. This is the aperture widening from "any
job" to "any year."

Kicker: `WHAT YOU'RE GOOD AT HAS A SHELF LIFE`

> Here's the part nobody says out loud. Some of what you get good at is going to stop mattering, and
> not because you did anything wrong. A specific tool, a specific workflow, the exact trick that
> made you fast: all of it has a shelf life now, and the shelf lives keep getting shorter. Right now
> you have almost nothing to unlearn. That's a real advantage, and it's temporary. Give it a few
> years and you'll be the one holding a method that used to work.

> So the skill isn't the method. It's what you do the week the method stops working. It looks like
> three habits: you try things before you know they'll work, you drop what you were good at when
> something better shows up, and you sit in not-knowing long enough to learn the new thing instead
> of retreating to the old one. That last one is the hard part. Being a beginner again, on purpose,
> is uncomfortable, and most people quit right there.

**Boundary note vs. Be Curious (next lesson):** Be Curious is about *reaching for* the new thing
(reps, habits, trying the feature the week it ships). This beat is about *letting go of* the old
thing (unlearning, ambiguity, being a beginner again). Neither one may drift into the other's verb.
If a later edit adds "try the new tool" to this beat, cut it; the next lesson owns it.

**Callback note:** "you have almost nothing to unlearn" deliberately extends Why Learn AI?'s
"Nothing to unlearn" card without naming the lesson. The audit confirmed these are different claims
(a head start you have now vs. a skill you'll need later). See judgment call 4.

### Beat E — Can we, and should we (SectionKicker + prose) — NEW

David's addition 3(b): ethical judgment. The widest circle: from you, to your year, to everyone the
work lands on. This is the last teaching beat, and the strongest place to end.

Kicker: `CAN WE, AND SHOULD WE`

> One more, and it's the one people skip. AI turned "can we build this" into a question with almost
> no friction. Scrape every public post from your class and rank them. Build a bot that texts in
> your voice. Automate a job three people currently do. The building isn't the hard part anymore.

> Which moves the whole question. "Can we" is answered. "Should we" isn't, and no model is going to
> raise its hand and ask it for you. It will hand you the plan. Someone in the room has to be the
> person who asks who this lands on, who never got a say, and what happens if it works exactly the
> way it's designed to.

> That isn't a rule you look up. It's a judgment you make, and you'll be making it earlier in your
> life than anyone before you had to.

**Dedupe note:** the audit confirmed Habits for the Road's five questions and Big Downside's worry
catalog both examine systems *someone else* built and pointed at you. This beat is the builder's
seat: a decision you make before the thing exists. Keep it in that seat; don't import the five
questions.

### Beat F — Closing thesis (SectionKicker + prose)

`HUMANEDGE_SURVIVORS: closing thesis`, adapted. "The skills above" is drawer language and gets
replaced; the tail now carries the shelf-life beat forward so the lesson lands as one argument.

Kicker: `AI MAKES PRODUCTION CHEAP.`

> That's what makes the rest of it expensive. None of what's on this page replaces AI. It's what
> makes AI worth using, and it's the part that stays yours no matter what ships next.

### Beat G — Close board, then capstone TRY IT

House order (matches `creativethinking`, `becurious`, `evaluating`): `closeBoard` → `InteractiveBox`
→ `LessonRule` → `NextLessonGate` (label "Next: Be Curious", target `becurious`).

**Header note:** propose a LessonHeader subtitle in the Creative Thinking mold, "What's left when
the typing is free."

## Close board

**Proposed (new):**

- pill: **"The typing got cheap."**
- sticky: **"The judgment didn't."**

**Why not the old pair.** The parked pair ("Some skills stay yours." / "That's where your value
compounds.") is good copy, but the very next lesson's board is "Curiosity compounds." / "The
questions you chase become your edge." Two consecutive boards leaning on *compounds* reads as an
echo, and *value compounds* is really the Make Your Move argument, which is where that pair's energy
belongs. The proposed pair lands this lesson's actual thesis in the lesson's own plainest words, and
matches the house shape of the neighbors (pill states what changed in the world, sticky states what
stayed yours). The old pair stays parked; see judgment call 3.

## Capstone TRY IT

**Resolution: one capstone. "Which Move Was Missing?" survives, extended to five items. "Spot the
Stronger Use of AI" retires to the parking lot.** This is not a close call, and here is why it
isn't:

- **The surviving one is bound to the load-bearing set.** The four move names must live in this
  lesson verbatim. `MISSING_SCENARIOS` is the only activity in the course that makes a student
  *name* one of them, which is exactly the skill a roundup should drill.
- **The retiring one collides with the lesson immediately after it.** Be Curious's "Curiosity or
  Outsourcing?" is mechanically and conceptually the same drill: two ways to use AI on the same
  situation, pick the one that keeps the thinking with the student. Running "Spot the Stronger Use
  of AI" and then "Curiosity or Outsourcing?" back to back is the exact repetition this rebuild
  exists to kill. The audit cleared it against `thoughtpartner` and `mindtrap`; it did not check it
  against the next lesson's activity. It should have.
- **Its four scenarios are still good.** Park them verbatim (essay on a novel not fully read /
  gap-year decision / fixing code you don't understand / poster design) in `docs/parking-lot.html`
  as donors. The poster scenario in particular is the best taste-and-prioritization rep in the
  course, and Make Your Move or AI Tips may want it.

**Mechanic:** unchanged from the survivor. `ScenarioRow` + `FeedbackPill`, parallel rows, all items
visible at once, two pills per item, per-pill feedback on click, no auto-reveal of the right answer.
Mint `surface`, `variant: "try"`.

**Item count: 5.** The four survivor scenarios verbatim, plus one new item.

| # | Scenario | Correct move | Distractor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vague study tips for guitar | Frame the real problem | Make sense of weird behavior |
| 2 | 21 + 22 + 19 + 10 = 68 | Make sense of weird behavior | Check what matters |
| 3 | "Should I major in CS or English?" | Make the human call | Frame the real problem |
| 4 | Snapchat My AI on Luka Dončić | Check what matters | Make sense of weird behavior |
| 5 | **NEW — the ethics item** | Make the human call | Frame the real problem |

Item 2's feedback keeps its verbatim line "it predicts the result instead of calculating it" — the
audit flags that claim as appearing nowhere else in the course.

**New item 5, proposed copy:**

> Setup: "Your group wants to build a class-ranking site: scrape every senior's public Instagram,
> score them on who looks most likely to get into a top school, post the list. You ask AI how to
> build it. It gives you a clean plan, step by step, and the plan would work."
>
> - **Frame the real problem** (wrong) — "More context would make it a better ranker. It wouldn't
>   make it a better idea. What's missing here isn't detail in the prompt."
> - **Make the human call** (correct) — "Right. AI answered 'can we.' Nobody in the room asked
>   'should we.' Every senior in that ranking is a person who never agreed to be scored, and the
>   plan works whether or not anyone thought about them."

**Skill it drills:** naming which human move was missing from a real AI interaction. Item 5 extends
the drill to the ethical-judgment cluster **without adding a fifth named move** — "should we build
this" is a human call, which is exactly what that card's body already claims ("it cannot own your
judgment, your values, your reputation, or the consequences of what you choose"). Two items sharing
one correct answer is a feature here: it shows one move covering two situations that look nothing
alike.

**Per-item feedback approach:** unchanged house pattern. The wrong pill explains why it was
tempting before it corrects, and the right pill's feedback teaches the mechanism rather than
congratulating. Every feedback line stays two sentences or under.

**What does not get drilled: adaptability.** The shelf-life beat can't be tested by a
scenario-naming mechanic, because it plays out over years, not inside one exchange. Any item that
tried would be a fake. It gets a body-copy beat and no drill, on purpose.

## SECTION_META proposal

```
skillsthatmatter: { kicker: "WHAT STAYS YOURS", label: "Skills That Matter", icon: "🧭" }
```

- **kicker:** `WHAT STAYS YOURS` (uppercase; sits alongside `FIND THE BETTER ANGLE` and
  `CURIOSITY COMPOUNDS`). Note the briefing's warning that kickers are often vestigial.
- **label:** `Skills That Matter` (approved name). No separate `title` needed; the label is short
  and unambiguous, matching `becurious`.
- **icon:** 🧭 (`🧭`). Unused anywhere in `SECTION_META` today, and it lands the
  production-to-direction thesis literally. It also appears on the "Make the human call" edge card
  inside this same lesson, which reads as an echo, not a collision. If David wants zero echo, the
  fallback is 🧗 (being a beginner again, the Beat D image); 🎯 is taken by `control`.

## Judgment calls for David

1. **Retire "Spot the Stronger Use of AI."** My recommendation, and the reasoning above is the
   Be Curious collision. The alternative is keeping both TRY ITs, which breaks the one-capstone norm
   *and* ships two near-identical drills in consecutive lessons. If you'd rather keep it, the better
   trade is to swap it in as the capstone and retire "Which Move Was Missing?" instead, which costs
   you the only place the four moves get practiced by name. I don't recommend that.
2. **Ethical judgment is drilled as "Make the human call," not as a fifth move.** This keeps the
   four names intact for Make Your Move's verbatim citation. The fork: if you want ethics to be its
   own named move ("Ask whether it should exist"), the set becomes five and Make Your Move's
   citation needs a matching edit. Cheap to do now, expensive later.
3. **New close board vs. the parked pair.** Proposed: "The typing got cheap." / "The judgment
   didn't." The parked pair ("Some skills stay yours." / "That's where your value compounds.") is
   still available and still good; my read is that *compounds* collides with Be Curious's board one
   lesson later, and that the value-compounds argument belongs to Make Your Move.
4. **The "nothing to unlearn" callback in Beat D.** As written it extends Why Learn AI?'s card
   without naming the lesson: you have almost nothing to unlearn, that's temporary. The alternative
   is naming the lesson explicitly for a harder callback (allowed mid-lesson, just not in the
   opener). Softer version is my default because this section is already dense with cross-lesson
   references.
