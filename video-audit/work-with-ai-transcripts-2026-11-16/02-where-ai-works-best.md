# 2. Where AI Works Best

`course-assets/where-ai-works-best/where-ai-works-best.mp4` · 2:53 · 491 words

**[0:00]** AI will take on almost anything you ask. You could ask it to brainstorm angles for an essay, plan a schedule, build code or draw an image. But what an AI can try to do is entirely different from what it is actually built for. We saw this firsthand building this course. AI successfully wrote the code for every page, earning an A+. But on the right side, when asked to draft actual lessons, it scored a C-. The explanations missed our intent, arranged points in the wrong order, and lacked a logical flow. The AI was excellent at testing whether a button worked or a page loaded correctly. But when a lesson required the right ideas presented in a way that made sense, the drafts weren't good enough to use. We still had to rely on our own judgment to decide what worked and what needed to change.

**[0:47]** Same AI, different jobs, different results. To get the best out of this tool, you need to recognize the four specific shapes of work it is actually strongest at handling.

**[0:57]** The first strength is reshape your material. Just provide something you already have and ask the AI to return it in a different format, preserving your original meaning. For example, take messy notes and have AI organize them into a clean study guide. Turn a scattered voice memo into a strict to-do list. The second strength is explore possibilities. When stuck, ask the AI for several approaches. You react to its suggestions, ask for variations, and decide which specific ideas are worth taking further. You might ask it to brainstorm distinct angles for an essay, or come up with ideas for a fundraiser.

**[1:34]** The third strength is find what matters. Supply the AI with long documents, and it will summarize core ideas or pull out the exact data points you requested. Locate specific requirements hidden in a scholarship application. Compare arguments made by two different articles. The fourth and final strength is work through problems. Tell AI what you're trying to accomplish, what you know, and what's in your way. It breaks the problem into manageable steps and compares possible approaches. Figure out an unexpected experiment result. Use this to plan a trip on a budget. AI organizes the logic, but doesn't make the final choice. All four of these strengths exist because of one underlying factor. Vast exposure. Training gave the AI exposure to more examples than any human could read in a lifetime.

**[2:20]** It ingested code, essays, explanations, emails, arguments, and conversations, making it highly fluent in common human formats. This massive exposure makes the AI extremely good at getting a task started. But it doesn't guarantee the answer is right or that it fits what you need. Because the system is ultimately predictive, you must apply your own judgment to evaluate the final context and accuracy. AI does some things better than others. Can try is not built for.
