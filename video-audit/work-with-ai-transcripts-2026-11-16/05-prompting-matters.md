# 5. Prompting Matters

`course-assets/prompting-matters/prompting-matters.mp4` · 3:28 · 572 words

**[0:00]** When Socrates asked his famous questions, he aimed every single one of them at a person. A person meets your question halfway. Your teacher already knows what class you're in, your friend knows what happened last weekend. An AI does not know those details, and you shouldn't assume it will ask for them. Asking an AI good questions requires giving it the specific information and instructions it needs to do its job. We call that prompting. Packaging your question for AI comes down to four specific moves. You don't need an entire class on prompt engineering to do this. You are simply learning to be smarter than the tool. This graphic breaks down the first two of those moves. Move one is share your situation. Do not make the AI guess what you're working on, who you are, or who the work is for.

**[0:48]** Tell it. Here is a weak prompt. What should my essay opening do? Compare that to a better approach. I'm a high school senior writing my Common App essay about how fixing cars with my dad taught me patience. What should the opening help the reader understand about me?

**[1:04]** Move to is give it the material. Give the AI the actual work you want help with, whether that is a draft, notes, an assignment rubric, specific numbers, or sources, not just a description of it. A weak version looks like this. Help me improve my essay intro. A better prompt provides the exact material. Here is my opening paragraph.

**[1:26]** Here is the essay question I'm answering.

**[1:29]** What is unclear or generic? These first two moves eliminate the AI's guesswork. Providing your context and source material turns a generic query into a highly personalized set of instructions. This next infographic outlines the final two moves, which dictate how the AI shapes and delivers its output. Move three is describe the answer you want. You need to specify the format, like a table or a list, set limits on length and tone, and provide an example of what you expect. A weak prompt leaves this completely open. Write a caption for our lacrosse championship photo. Compare that to a better approach. Write a caption for our team's photo after the lacrosse state championship. One sentence. No hashtags, no emojis, nothing corporate.

**[2:17]** Sound like a senior wrote it, not the school account.

**[2:21]** Move4 is one job at a time. Break big work into distinct steps, and tell the AI exactly where to stop and wait for your review before building on it. A weak approach asks for everything at once. Write me a five-page paper on the Cold War with an outline, thesis, research, MLA citations, and a conclusion. A better prompt isolates the immediate task. Help me shape a strong thesis for a five-page paper on how the space race reflected Cold War tensions. Let's work on the thesis first. A quick factual question requires zero packaging. You just ask it. Real work, like an essay or a project, requires sharing your situation, giving the tool your material, and describing the answer you want. And massive, complex projects always require you to implement Move 4, breaking the process down one job at a time.

**[3:12]** This graphic summarizes the final rule regarding the effort you put into a prompt versus the outcome you receive. A prompt is a briefing, not magic words. The more the result matters, the more you bring.
