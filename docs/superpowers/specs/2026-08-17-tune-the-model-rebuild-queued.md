**The reframe (David's):** the lesson was written as "choose the model." The key idea is

## New front half (replaces the current opening + hockey-line tier teaching)

**Opening** (David's sketch, punctuation pass):

> ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini are all great choices for your go-to AI app. Now comes
> something normal apps never ask of you. Spotify doesn't ask which Spotify you want.
> AI apps do: you get choices about which model does the work, how fast it answers,
> and how hard it thinks.

**Dealership analogy** (David's two paragraphs + a one-line mapping):

> Think about buying a new car. Your first decision is which dealership to visit:
> Ford, Chevy, Dodge, or somewhere else. When you arrive, the next choice is which
> model you want to buy.
>
> Next come the options: engine size, wheel package, trim. And you'll pick those
> based on how you plan to use the car.
>
> AI works the same way. The app was the dealership, and you've already picked it.
> The choices waiting inside are the model, the speed, and the thinking.

**SectionKicker: YOUR CHOICES** — intro (from David's notes):

> The choices live inside whichever app you picked. What makes them confusing is the
> names: each app names its models differently, and with every app improving this
> fast, the names keep changing. So don't memorize names. Learn the three choices
> underneath them. Those don't change.

**Three boxes** (modeled on Pace of Change's accelerant-box anatomy; internal headings
Description / Reason / When to use; drafts below are for David's edit):

**Box 1 — MODEL**
- Description: A family of models, not one brain · The picker lists them by name,
  newest near the top · The labels carry the signal: one is marked most capable, one
  is the everyday default
- Reason: Bigger models reason deeper and cost more to run. Smaller ones are cheap
  enough to answer everything else. No company can give everyone the biggest brain
  for every question, so the picker exists.
- When to use: The default handles most days. Switch up when the work is hard, long,
  or graded.

**Box 2 — SPEED**
- Description: Trimmed versions of the brain, built to answer fast · Short names that
  sound quick: mini, flash, lite · Some apps list them as their own models, others
  fold them into one menu
- Reason: A quick question doesn't need the deepest brain. Fast versions trade some
  depth for speed, and on most quick asks you'll never notice what they traded.
- When to use: Quick lookups, simple rewrites, anything you'd be fine asking twice.
  When the answer matters more than the wait, move up.

**Box 3 — THINKING**
- Description: A switch that lets the model work longer before it answers · Named
  things like extended thinking, reasoning, think longer · Same brain, more time
- Reason: Hard problems need more than one pass. Thinking time lets the model try
  approaches, check its own work, and throw out weak drafts before you ever see one.
- When to use: Math, code, planning, anything with steps that build on each other.
  Skip it for quick facts: you'll wait longer for the same answer.
