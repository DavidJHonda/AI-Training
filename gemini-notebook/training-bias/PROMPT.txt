Create a video for high school students from training-bias.md and the three attached boards.

REQUIRED VERBATIM AUDIO
“You heard three perspectives. Imagine an AI learned from only one.”
“The list tells you which industry showed up. You’d need more evidence to know why.”
“What’s missing from this answer?”
“Show me examples that don’t fit the pattern you just gave.”
“Answer again, leaving out the most famous examples.”
“AI learns from its training data.”
“Bias in the data can shape the answer.”

TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Open with the exam question. Voice the science teacher’s three hours, the mom’s 30 minutes because the student works tonight, and the best friend’s “Studying is overrated.” Explain how experience shapes their advice. Use the three-perspectives bridge to introduce an imaginary AI trained on study advice only from science teachers’ websites. Show how repeated three-hour advice before exams shapes its answer. Teach both sides of Types of Training Bias. Connect Shows Up Too Often to three hours and Gets Left Out to the scarcity of examples of how students with evening shifts study. The model’s training sources are known in this hypothetical.

Move to how possible bias feels in everyday use. The question asks about successful entrepreneurs; the answer lists ten tech founders. Voice the student’s reaction and follow-up about other industries. Explain what the list reveals and what its cause would require evidence to establish. Walk through the three prompts on When You Suspect Training Bias in order. Follow-ups can broaden an answer; never promise the model has the missing perspective. End with the exact closing lines.

VOICE
Speak directly to the student. Use the dialogue, pace, and everyday words in the lesson. Give the responses to the exam question without asking viewers to guess. Keep the lab, quiz, and production directions out of the narration.

BOARDS AND VISUALS
Start with the student’s exam question, without an opening title card. Use drawn scenes for the teacher, mom, friend, and entrepreneur list. Show each attached board complete and uncropped as its content is spoken. No photos or photorealistic imagery. Illustrated, cartoon, and stylized people are allowed. Do not reuse the cow footage, old category names, rest-of-the-picture banner, or training-cutoff ending. Avoid invented statistics, logos, chapter cards, and spoken URLs. Finish on the closing board, with nothing after its final line.
