 112.18- 114.38  Here are three ways to fight the flattery trap.
 115.14- 117.22  The first move is ask, don't tell.
 118.10- 121.94  When you hand over a project, share the work itself, but hold back your verdict.
 122.52- 125.68  If you tell the AI you think a plan is good, it will likely agree.
 126.28- 127.72  Ask for an evaluation first.
 128.36- 131.52  If you just drop a paper into a chat and ask, is my essay good,
 131.94- 134.72  you are handing the AI a broad request for approval.
 134.72- 139.24  It will gladly say yes, leaving you with zero direction on what to actually revise.
 140.12- 143.14  Instead, use the second move, ask for the gaps.
 143.64- 146.06  Give the AI specific flaws to hunt for.
 146.62- 151.12  Ask what is weak, what is missing, and what a critic would say to disagree with your argument.
 151.72- 156.22  By directly requesting objections, you override the model's default setting for agreement
 156.22- 158.94  and force it to highlight the areas that need your attention.
 159.58- 161.64  The third move is use a rubric.
 161.64- 165.62  Without a clear standard, AI evaluates using invisible criteria.
 166.24- 170.52  Instead, provide the rubric, demand it, quote, specific evidence from your text,
 170.86- 174.48  and ask it to explain exactly how to move up one grading level.
 174.90- 177.78  The response you get is a starting point for revisions,
 178.16- 181.96  not a guaranteed grade or an official ruling on your work's quality.
 182.62- 186.54  To automate this, you can save a standing instruction in your app's settings.
 187.16- 190.08  Tell the AI to be direct, to lead with what needs work,
 190.08- 192.72  and to explicitly point out when you are wrong.
 193.74- 196.36  All three of these strategies serve one clear purpose,
 196.96- 200.98  instructing the AI to evaluate the work rather than validate the user.
 201.72- 204.38  Even when the help is useful, there's another question.
 205.08- 206.64  Did you choose to keep going?
 207.42- 209.58  Accepting extra assistance feels productive,
 209.98- 214.26  but continuing the conversation without deciding to do so is a trap of its own.

 255.76- 258.86  The engagement trap is spending time you never decided to spend.
 259.56- 265.12  It works because a helpful follow-up instantly places the next task directly in front of you,
 265.56- 269.62  short-circuiting your ability to pause and decide if you actually need it.
 270.44- 272.66  Look for specifics, not approval.
 273.40- 274.42  Know what you came for.
 274.42- 277.24  When you have it, choose what happens next.
