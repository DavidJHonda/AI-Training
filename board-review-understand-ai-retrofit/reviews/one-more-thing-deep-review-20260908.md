## First recommendation: explain continuity without denying memory categorically

“AI doesn’t remember,” “It doesn’t,” and “AI does not even remember the last word it wrote” are stronger than the explanation supports. Chatbot applications have persistent memory features; models also use stored numerical state while generating. Human episodic memory is not the right technical boundary for claiming there is no memory at all. Context Window already describes working memory and saved notes, creating an internal course conflict.

Focus this section on how earlier conversation information is made available as context. Suggested kicker: “How AI Follows the Conversation.” Suggested paragraph after the dog setup: “AI can use the earlier discussion because the app includes it in the context for your new question. The model uses that information to see that you chose a dog and help you name it.” Review the following “does not even remember” paragraph and the board’s categorical human-versus-AI language as part of the same topic. Saved app memory may warrant a short sentence; avoid turning this into a product-settings lesson. Do not imply the entire transcript is always resent or reread from scratch: history can be selected, summarized, or cached.

Primary support: [OpenAI memory documentation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8590148-memory-faq), [Claude chat search and memory](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11817273-use-claude-s-chat-search-and-memory-to-build-on-previous-context), [Gemini past-chat memory](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16598469), and [Transformers caching](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/cache_explanation). Read as of review date. Do not freeze plan availability or implementation specifics into the lesson.

## Other substantive recommendations, held for discussion

1. **Compute assumptions.** One trillion total weights does not imply one trillion weights are used for each token. A documented counterexample is Qwen3-235B-A22B: 235 billion total, 22 billion active. The current two-calculations-per-weight arithmetic can illustrate a hypothetical dense model, but it cannot estimate a proprietary chatbot from total model size alone. Prefer a clearly hypothetical worked example or a published architecture; retain impressive scale without claiming the user’s real dog chat cost two quadrillion operations. [Qwen model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-235B-A22B).
2. **Scope of the computation count.** The board’s “Complete Dog Chat” is actually an assumed 1,000 generated tokens times a rough per-token weight computation. It excludes prompt processing and does not account for all attention costs or hidden reasoning. The visible lesson also does not show a 1,000-token chat. A label such as “A Longer Conversation” and explicit generated-token assumption is more defensible. The activity lead calls this “your complete dog chat,” which also needs alignment. Do not replace one uncertain exact number with another.
3. **Standalone introduction.** “Across this section, you built the machine” assumes prior lessons. The three motivating questions can carry the introduction by themselves. Preserve the conversational “One more thing. Actually, three.” if the user likes it. “Answer those, and the whole process comes together” is expendable.
4. **Sampling language.** The weighted drawing correctly explains stochastic selection, but “safest token” means highest probability here, not safety or correctness. Prefer “most likely token.” “Answers would start to sound repetitive” should be a tendency rather than a universal consequence. No need to teach temperature/top-p now. [Generation strategies](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/generation_strategies) distinguishes greedy choice and sampling and describes repetition/diversity tradeoffs.
5. **Same Odds, Five Draws.** The probabilities sum to 100: 22+17+14+9+6+32. The 32% Other category is a group, not one candidate; the striped bar helps. Spot has a 22% individual chance and all alternatives 78%. The five listed outcomes are valid examples, not a measured frequency claim. Clarify only if necessary that these are five separate attempts at the same answer position, not five consecutive tokens. The same-question/prefix framing matters because new prefixes change the distribution.
6. **Repetition before the lottery board.** Several paragraphs explain tickets, the leading candidate, alternatives, and diversity before a board that repeats those ideas. Let the board carry numerical comparisons after explaining the ticket analogy. Do not cut the important distinction between the most likely single token and the combined alternatives.
7. **Terminology consistency.** “Scores” reappears even though How AI Answers now teaches probabilities directly. “Words” in the last-word memory paragraph also reverses the token terminology. Once the memory section is rewritten, align these references without reteaching tokenization.
8. **Closing statement.** “Not a mind” is a broader philosophical claim than the mechanisms establish and repeats the human-versus-AI framing. A concrete closing about predictions, context, and scale would better summarize the actual learning. The second line “Every time you hit send” can remain if retained by the user.
9. **Long chats.** “Can make long conversations slower” is appropriately qualified. Explain available context without saying all prior computations always repeat; caches reuse earlier work. The practical suggestion to start a new chat for a different task is reasonable, but “more text ... kept available” must not imply an unlimited permanent context window.

## TRY IT arithmetic and humor

Checked using the dimensions already assumed in the text, not verified physical measurements of an actual burger or arbitrary coin:

- 2 quadrillion burgers at 2.5 inches: 78.914 billion miles. About 80 billion and roughly 21 times 3.7 billion miles are consistent approximations. “Past Pluto, twenty times over” is a playful scale comparison, not an orbital distance calculation.
- 2 quadrillion coins at 1.5 mm: 1.864 billion miles, approximately ten Sun-and-back trips using the ordinary Earth–Sun distance. Coin thickness is an assumption.
- 2 quadrillion 12 US fl oz cans: 709.765 cubic km. Relative to Lake Erie’s 484 cubic km, 1.466 reservoirs, appropriately rounded to one and a half. [EPA Great Lakes data](https://www.epa.gov/greatlakes/physical-features-great-lakes).
- Consumption over 100 years at 365.25 days/year: 54.757 billion cans/day, 633,762/second. The stated 55 billion and 634,000 are consistent.
- **Horse claim not validated.** “500 times all life on Earth” mixes unspecified average horse mass and unspecified biomass basis. The primary global biomass estimate is about 550 gigatons of carbon, which cannot be directly compared with horses’ total wet mass. At an assumed 500 kg per horse the numerator is 1e18 kg; no supported denominator for the stated 500 ratio is provided. Do not call the ratio technically true without a defensible mass basis. [PNAS biomass study](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1711842115). Nature’s dry/wet biomass paper was found but full access failed; do not claim it validates the ratio.
- The horse question explicitly marks the numerical choice wrong while saying it is true. “Choose the estimate you think is closest” therefore conflicts with the intended punchline “Lots, and who cares?” Preserve humor but make the rule or final-item joke clear. This is a framing issue, not a reason to remove the mental break.
