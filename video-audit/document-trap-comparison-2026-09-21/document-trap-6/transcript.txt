[0:41.00-0:43.00] This is the document trap.
[0:43.00-0:49.00] We assume that because a file is uploaded, the AI has fully read and comprehended every single word inside it.
[0:49.00-0:53.00] An AI can only answer based on the text that fits inside its context window.
[0:53.00-0:55.00] A short file might drop in perfectly.
[0:55.00-1:01.00] But with a long file, the system has to filter the text and search for the parts that seem most relevant.

[1:06.00-1:08.00] This breaks down the workflow.
[1:08.00-1:11.00] First is split, breaking your document into smaller pieces.
[1:11.00-1:16.00] Second is search, finding pieces matching your question based on keywords and meaning.
[1:16.00-1:20.00] Third is load, putting selected pieces into the context window.
[1:20.00-1:24.00] Search acts as a strict gatekeeper, deciding which parts reach the answer.
[1:24.00-1:27.00] This is exactly how the rulebook mistake happens.

[1:40.00-1:43.00] Retrieval, Augmented Generation, or RAG.
[1:43.00-1:48.00] The exact same mechanism is used any time an AI pulls information from a large database or the open web.
[1:48.00-1:54.00] When retrieval misses a critical condition or exception, the OI lacks the context to answer accurately.
[1:54.00-1:59.00] It provides a confident, detailed response that is factually incomplete.
[1:59.00-2:02.00] Guide the AI's retrieval with four precise moves.

[2:06.00-2:10.00] Second, ask one thing, giving retrieval a single target.
[2:10.00-2:16.00] Third, share what matters by manually pasting relevant passages directly into your prompt.
[2:16.00-2:21.00] Fourth, ask for the quote to compare its answer against the original text.
