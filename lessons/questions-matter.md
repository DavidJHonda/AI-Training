## WORK WITH AI

# Questions Matter

AI can help with all kinds of work. But getting useful help starts with knowing what to ask.

For decades, technology has been making it easier and easier to get answers. The less time we spend chasing an answer, the more time we can spend asking the right question.

One example makes the whole idea click. Teachers have been handing out research assignments since the dawn of school. But look at how this changed between your grandparents, your parents, and you.

### Board 1: How Answers Got Easier and Faster

**Image file:** `questions-matter-answers-faster.jpg`

![How Answers Got Easier and Faster](questions-matter-answers-faster.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

The Library: travel there. Search the catalog. Track down books. Read and take notes by hand. Time to answer: half a Saturday.

Search: run searches. Open tabs. Judge which sites to trust. Copy the useful pieces. Time to answer: an hour or two.

AI: open the app. Ask. The answer appears on your screen. Time to answer: seconds.

Answers became cheap to get.

Half a day, then an hour, now seconds. AI makes answers cheap to get. That does not make humans less valuable. **It changes where value lives.**

### Board 2: It Changes Where Value Lives

**Image file:** `questions-matter-value-lives.jpg`

![It Changes Where Value Lives](questions-matter-value-lives.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

Pre-AI: finding the answer. Finding a good answer took work. You had to know where to look, what to trust, and what mattered.

With AI: asking the right question. AI can give you an answer in seconds. You still have to decide which questions to ask and whether the answer helps.

Answers got cheap. Questions didn’t.

## What Makes a Good Question?

Asking good questions is one of humanity’s oldest skills. Socrates taught by doing almost nothing but asking. The scientific method doesn’t start with an answer; it starts with a question worth testing. Someone once said that with an hour to save the world, they’d spend the first 55 minutes finding the right question.

So what separates a good question from a weak one? Four qualities, and none of them mention AI.

### Board 3A: Four Qualities of a Good Question

**Image file:** `questions-matter-open-minded-and-specific.jpg`

![Four Qualities of a Good Question: Open-Minded and Specific](questions-matter-open-minded-and-specific.jpg)

**Teaching content:**

Open-Minded: you haven’t picked the answer in advance. A leading question isn’t research, it’s a request for backup. Weak: “Homework doesn’t help students learn. Can you help me prove it?” Better: “What does the research actually say about homework and learning?”

Specific: give enough detail about your situation to get an answer that fits. Weak: “How do I get better at sports?” Better: “I play basketball as a point guard. Sometimes I lose the ball against pressure. What drills fix that?”

### Board 3B: Four Qualities of a Good Question, Continued

**Image file:** `questions-matter-on-target-and-open-ended.jpg`

![Four Qualities of a Good Question, Continued: On Target and Open-Ended](questions-matter-on-target-and-open-ended.jpg)

On Target: ask about the problem you actually need to solve. The first question that comes to mind might miss it. Weak: “What energy drinks work best for staying awake in class?” Better: “I keep falling asleep in first period, even when the class is interesting. What could be making me so tired, and what can I change?”

Open-Ended: invite an explanation, not just a yes or no. Leave room for an answer you didn’t expect. Weak: “Should I join the debate team?” Better: “What would joining debate add to my week, and what would I have to give up for it?”
