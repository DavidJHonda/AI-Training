# Engagement Trap: Meta teen-safety settlement source record

Reviewed September 19, 2026 for the lesson addition and the board **Putting the Stopping Points Back**.

## Student-facing claims

- **Money:** State attorney general summaries describe the main multistate settlement as up to **$17.1 billion**. Meta describes the agreement as approximately **$18 billion**, paid in annual installments over ten years. Meta says about 70 percent, approximately $12.7 billion, is allocated over the decade; the remaining 30 percent, approximately $5.3 billion, is released only if TikTok and YouTube both adopt specified protections and make matching payments. The lesson therefore describes the higher number as an approximate, conditional maximum rather than an unconditional fine already paid.
- **Claims and non-admission:** The states alleged that Meta used addictive design and harmed children and teens. The consent judgment says Meta denies the allegations and liability, and the settlement is not an admission of wrongdoing.
- **Who is covered:** The product requirements apply to under-18 accounts on Facebook and Instagram in participating U.S. states and territories.
- **Daily limit:** The initial requirement is a default two-hour cumulative daily limit across Facebook and Instagram. A teen can turn it off only with a parent’s permission. The stronger one-hour-per-app requirement is contingent on industry peers joining and is not used on the board.
- **Prompts:** Teens receive prompts after each 15 minutes of continuous use and when total daily use reaches 60 and 90 minutes. The board calls these **Required Pauses** but describes them as prompts, avoiding an unsupported claim that each prompt locks the apps.
- **Night mode:** The initial requirement is a default block from midnight to 6 a.m. Meta says posting and viewing Feed, Stories, Explore, and Reels are blocked. Direct messages are excluded from Night Mode, Time Limit, and School Mode. The board therefore says the block limits **most** app use.
- **Implementation wording:** Meta’s announcement says the judge approved the agreement on August 27, 2026 and that the protections will automatically apply in participating jurisdictions. The lesson describes the features as required changes and does not claim they are already active for every student.
- **Scope:** The settlement concerns social media. The lesson uses it as a design comparison for deliberate stopping decisions in AI chat; it does not claim the same harms or legal requirements apply to AI chatbots.
