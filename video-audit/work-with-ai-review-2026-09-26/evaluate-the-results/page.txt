WORK WITH AI
Evaluate the Results
▸

AI makes mistakes. It uses the same confident voice whether the facts are right or wrong. But even an answer with no factual mistakes can fall short. A joke that isn’t funny. An explanation that leaves you confused. A plan that won’t work.

One option is to ignore AI. Pretend it doesn’t exist. Nobody who’s made it this far in the course is doing that: AI is too powerful to leave on the shelf. To Be Smarter Than the Tool, you need the right skills to evaluate AI’s results.

When you evaluate an answer, ask two questions: Is it right? Is it good enough for what I need?

EVERY RESPONSE: THE QUICK PASS

Start with these three steps before you use an AI answer.

The Quick Pass
Read. Read the answer before you use it. If you pass it along, you’re responsible for what it says.
Understand. You can’t judge an answer you don’t understand. Ask AI, “Explain the second paragraph in simpler terms.”
Validate. Compare the answer with what you know and what you asked for. Is anything wrong, missing, or not useful?

Before you use it: read, understand, validate.

THEN DECIDE: DOES THIS ANSWER DESERVE THE DIG?

Some answers need a closer look. These three questions help you decide.

Do You Need to Dig Deeper?
Can You Judge It?. Compare the answer with what you know. If you don’t know enough to judge it, keep checking.
What Kind of Task Is It?. A factual answer needs accurate information. A draft needs clear writing. A plan needs to work. Check what matters for the task.
How Much Is Riding on It?. A movie pick is low stakes. A scholarship application or advice about an injury deserves more care. What happens if the answer is wrong?

Give the answer the attention it deserves.

WHEN IT DESERVES THE DIG

When an answer needs a closer look, choose the checks that fit the task.

Dig Deeper
Check the Sources. Ask for sources, then open them. Does each source support the claim? Is it reliable?
Challenge the Answer. Ask, “What’s the strongest argument against this?” Look for weaknesses you hadn’t considered.
Ask What’s Missing. Ask, “What important information did you leave out?” Decide whether it changes your view of the answer.
Search the Live Web. Ask AI to search the web for current information. Open the sources and check what they say.
Check It Yourself. Find another way to check the answer. Recalculate the numbers, test the code, or ask someone who knows the subject.

Use AI to help you check. You decide whether the answer holds up.

THEN MAKE YOUR MOVE

Now decide what to do with the answer.

Make Your Move
Use It. The answer is right and good enough for what you need. Put it to work.
Fix It. Tell AI what needs to change, or change it yourself. Then check the revised answer before you use it.
Walk Away. If AI isn’t helping you get there, try another approach or ask someone who knows the subject.

01
YOU ASKED
Suggest a movie for a group of friends who like Marvel movies.
For tonight’s movie night, you can’t go wrong with Avengers: Endgame. Epic battles, big laughs, and the payoff to a decade of movies: it plays just as well with four people as with fourteen.
Good to go
✓
CORRECT
Right. It fits your group.
You asked for a movie for friends who like Marvel. This fits, and you can decide whether it sounds fun. That’s enough to make the choice.
Dig deeper
✕
NOT QUITE
A quick check is enough here.
You don’t need to prove this is the best movie. You need something your friends will enjoy. If it fits the group, pick it. If it doesn’t, ask for another suggestion.
02
YOU ASKED
Find a scholarship I could apply for as a high-school senior.
You’re in luck! In-N-Out Burger runs the Animal Style Scholars Program: $20,000 awarded to 50 high-school seniors every year. Applications close March 1, and the essay prompt is about secret menus. Want me to start your draft?
Good to go
✕
NOT QUITE
Check before you apply.
Before you spend time on the essay, find the scholarship on the organization’s official website. Check that it exists, that you qualify, and that the deadline is current.
Dig deeper
✓
CORRECT
Right. Details need evidence.
A convincing description isn’t enough. Find the scholarship on the organization’s official website and check the requirements and deadline. You don’t want to write an essay for a scholarship AI invented.
03
YOU ASKED
I twisted my ankle at practice. What should I do?
For a sprained ankle, follow R.I.C.E.: rest, ice for 20 minutes at a time, compression with an elastic bandage, and elevation above your heart. Stay off it for 48 hours, then ease back in as the pain allows.
Good to go
✕
NOT QUITE
How does AI know it’s a sprain?
The advice sounds familiar, but it assumes you have a sprain. That hasn’t been established. Tell a parent or athletic trainer what happened and get help deciding what care you need.
Dig deeper
✓
CORRECT
Right. Check the assumption.
Advice for a sprain only helps if it fits your injury. A confident answer doesn’t establish that. This is a time to involve someone who can assess the ankle, rather than rely on the chat.
04
YOU ASKED
Give me five ideas to help our team’s car wash raise more money.
Five ideas for the team car-wash fundraiser: pre-sell tickets at lunch, add a “coach gets pied” stretch goal, offer a monthly pass, challenge the rival school to a wash-off, and film before-and-after clips for the team account.
Good to go
✓
CORRECT
Good starting points.
You asked for ideas, and AI gave you options. Pick the ones that fit your team, budget, and time. You don’t need a research project to choose a few favorites.
Dig deeper
✕
NOT QUITE
What needs a closer look?
A quick check is enough to pick a few promising ideas. Before you put one into action, work out the details. A “coach gets pied” event, for example, needs a willing coach.
05
YOU ASKED
Suggest a quote about how people have different abilities for the opening of my essay.
Here’s a strong opener for your essay. As Albert Einstein said, “Everybody is a genius. But if you judge a fish by its ability to climb a tree, it will live its whole life believing that it is stupid.”
Good to go
✕
NOT QUITE
Did Einstein say it?
A quote can make a great opening, but the name attached to it needs checking. Look for a reliable source that identifies where and when the person said or wrote it. Seeing the same attribution on several quote websites isn’t enough.
Dig deeper
✓
CORRECT
Right. Check who said it.
AI gave you two things: a quote and a claim about its author. Before you put Einstein’s name in your essay, verify that claim. If you can’t, choose a quote you can source or make the point in your own words.
