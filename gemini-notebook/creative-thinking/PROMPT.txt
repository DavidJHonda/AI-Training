TEACH THE COMPLETE LESSON
Open with the “Good idea!” hook and explain why someone says it: you saw something missed, connected things differently, or found a better way. Define creative thinking. Explain that it extends beyond artists, writers, and musicians. Keep the Steve Jobs example: computers could be simpler, more personal, and easier to use. Teach all four professions with their complete explanations: lawyer, entrepreneur, engineer, doctor. Keep the lawyer’s same-laws-and-facts comparison. Explain that creativity is a thinking habit beneath any job where the standard answer is not enough.

Teach the whole Why It Matters passage: polished AI answers are a beginning; the learner notices omissions, connects ideas, and chooses a direction. Do not claim everyone using the same AI gets similar or identical answers, that output has zero variance, that a baseline is equalized, or that advantage moves entirely to one person. Creativity is not an inborn gift reserved for some people; its habits can improve.

Teach all four habits by name with their full instructions: Generate Before You Judge, including bad ideas and the fact that obvious ideas usually arrive first; Ask “What If?”, changing a rule or assumption and considering its opposite; Connect Unrelated Things, borrowing and applying an approach; Step Away, Then Return, working first and allowing new connections afterward. Explain options before judgment, then speak both closing lines in order. Do not narrate the career LAB or add a recap.
