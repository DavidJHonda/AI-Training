## Suggested changes / reroll priorities

1. **0:43–0:48 — Remove “almost certainly” gives glowing praise.** The example can demonstrate the failure without claiming it is nearly inevitable.
2. **0:53–0:59 — Correct the claim that the essay never named the theme.** It explicitly names the American Dream. The actual problem is vague filler and no thesis. Preserve that distinction and the useful critique afterward. The current lesson’s “It praises a theme it never named” is ambiguous: it can refer to the AI’s generic praise, not the essay. The video turns the ambiguity into a definite incorrect claim. Clarify the source wording before generating again; the current illustrated board already focuses on unsupported praise and the missing thesis.
3. **1:20–1:27 and 2:09–2:17 — Remove the design/hardwiring overstatement.** “Designed to flatter rather than critique” and “hardwired…agreement over truth” are stronger than the lesson’s conditional explanation. Human preferences can reward agreeableness; that is not the sole or unchangeable design goal. [OpenAI’s postmortem](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/) describes interacting changes and feedback signals, not a single permanent hardwiring.
4. **3:00–3:11 — Keep the rollback fact, not the implied single-example cause.** The broader sycophancy problem prompted the rollback; the poop-on-a-stick response is an illustration of it. OpenAI says rollout began April 25 and rollback began April 28. [OpenAI’s timeline](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/)
5. **3:31–3:53 — Give the first four moves more substance.** They pass in roughly 22 seconds; the rubric gets only “forces a rigid standard.” Students need a usable prompt and the reason each change helps. The existing board carries this detail, but showing a dense board during rapid narration is not the same as teaching it. This is the biggest reroll reason. Prioritize neutral question → less opinion to echo; gaps → change the feedback task; rubric → evidence against a standard; other side → expose hidden weaknesses.
6. **3:25–3:30, 4:05–4:11, and 4:12–4:23 — Remove promises of a prompting cure.** “Force…objective evaluation,” “strip away…ability,” “permanently,” and “exact override” imply these prompts solve sycophancy. They help; they do not guarantee honesty. Keep the actual standing instruction at **4:24–4:29**.
