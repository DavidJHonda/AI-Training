# The Tail of Hanoi

A true story from the course lesson “Unexpected Results.”

## Hanoi, 1902

The city had a big problem. There were too many rats in the new sewers. The sewers had just been built as part of a huge modernization project, and their dark, protected tunnels turned out to be a perfect place for rats to breed. Rats spilled out of the grates and into the streets.

The government devised a simple, logical plan. Turn in a severed rat’s tail, collect a payment of one cent. Tails poured in by the thousands, day after day. By every metric the government tracked, the plan was a brilliant success.

Then people saw something very strange: rats running around the city, perfectly alive, with no tails.

## What actually happened

It was a math thing. Rats can live a relatively full rat life without their tails, and keep having rat babies. The rat hunters realized the rules paid for tails, not for dead rats. They clipped the tails, collected the cash, and released the living rats back into the sewers to breed. A dead rat pays once. A live rat pays forever.

Some rat entrepreneurs went further. They stopped hunting in the sewers entirely and started rat farms outside the city, raising rats just to harvest their tails. The government was unknowingly funding a private rat-production industry.

When officials discovered the farms, they abruptly canceled the bounty. The farmers released their now-worthless rats into the wild. Hanoi ended up with a mountain of rat tails, depleted funds, and more rats than when the program started.

## The lesson of the story: the unexpected result

This story is history’s clearest example of an **unexpected result**: an action creates an outcome different from, or opposite to, the intended goal.

Every part of the government’s plan worked. People hunted rats, delivered tails, and received their payments. The plan worked. The goal failed. The boom in the rat population was the opposite of what the plan was for.

They only paid for tails.
